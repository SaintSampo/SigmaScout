## worker-tick-exceeds-cpu-budget — every cron tick killed with `exceededCpu` after a data republish opened phantom "live" windows
- **Date:** 2026-08-29
- **Error patterns:** outcome exceededCpu, cpuTime pinned at 10, empty logs array, wallTime >> cpuTime, 100% of cron ticks failing, worker healthy for a week then failing with no deploy, Cloudflare Workers free plan CPU budget, Zod validation on hot path, inferred live window, zero-match offseason event
- **Root cause(s):** AND-gate, two simultaneous conditions, neither sufficient alone. (A) [code] `runTick` answered "is anything live?" by Zod-validating the ENTIRE 1,581-window / 160 KB live-windows manifest (~7,900 field validations) before filtering — 1,542 of those windows were permanently-closed past seasons. A 1-minute cron lands on an evicted isolate nearly every tick, so it always paid the COLD price (3.4–3.9 ms desktop / 5–9 ms deployed), meaning a DO-NOTHING tick already consumed 50–90% of the 10 ms budget; (B) [data] `buildLiveWindowsManifest` synthesised a blind 4-day `inferred:true` window from `start_date` for any event with zero matches. A full republish on 2026-08-28 put offseason events in scope, opening two PHANTOM windows for events that were not running and had no schedule — so the tick stopped taking its `liveEvents.length === 0` early exit and ran a 38 ms live path on top of (A), on every tick.
- **Fix:** A1 filter-at-read — new `loadLiveEventsAt` validates only the manifest ENVELOPE, structurally prefilters raw entries on `startMs`/`endMs`, then schema-parses ONLY live entries (undecidable entries still hard-fail via `LiveWindowShapeError`). A2 shrink-at-build — `buildLiveWindowsManifest` drops windows already closed at `computedAt` (clock-injected, not `Date.now()`). B no-blind-windows — the zero-match fallback is removed entirely; an event is live only if its matches are in the corpus. A1 alone cleared the outage on deploy; A2 and B land on the next republish and prevent recurrence.
- **Files changed:** apps/worker/src/liveWindows.ts, apps/worker/src/scheduled.ts, packages/harness/manifestSchemas.ts, packages/harness/manifests.ts, apps/worker/test/liveWindows.test.ts, packages/harness/manifests.test.ts, docs/worker-operations.md, .planning/WINDOWS.md
- **Why not caught:** No gate existed for "a resource budget is nearly exhausted by the do-nothing path". Three softer gates were available and all passed. (1) `docs/worker-operations.md` RECORDED idle ticks at 5–9 ms of a 10 ms budget on 2026-08-22/23 and published them as a healthy baseline — nobody asked what the remaining 1–5 ms was meant to pay for. (2) The same run logged a 14 ms cold start returning `ok:true`, which the team's flat-10 ms model made IMPOSSIBLE; it was written down as an open question rather than chased, a week before the outage. (3) Liveness was verified against operator knowledge ("is a real competition happening?") instead of against the manifest the Worker actually reads, so "no event was live" was asserted while two were — that false premise misdirected the first investigation into module init for hours.
- **Recurrence guard:** `packages/harness/manifests.test.ts` — outage-shaped test asserting a zero-match offseason event is not live at ANY of the 96 hourly instants its guessed window used to span, plus the `endMs == nowMs` / `endMs - 1` retention boundary pair and an envelope/full-schema lockstep guard (7 of these fail against the pre-fix builder, verified by revert). `apps/worker/test/liveWindows.test.ts` — 18 tests including an equivalence check against the old full-parse path across 12 boundary instants and a 1,581-window outage-shaped case whose 1,579 non-live entries are deliberately schema-invalid; two independent implementation mutations confirmed caught. Plus `docs/worker-operations.md`'s new "How the CPU budget is actually enforced" section and the ingest-before-live operational contract ("read the manifest, never the calendar").
- **Method lessons (transferable, not specific to this bug):**
  - **A resource limit is not always a flat ceiling — establish the enforcement model, not just the constant.** This session assumed Cloudflare's free plan kills any invocation at exactly 10 ms. It does not: 10 ms is the CONFIGURED limit, but each isolate has documented "built-in flexibility" for INFREQUENT overruns and terminates only when the Worker "starts hitting the limit consistently". Proven from this project's own data — the same version returned `ok` at cpuTime 38 and was killed at cpuTime 10 sixty seconds later; successes are recorded up to 208 ms; kills are pinned at exactly 10 on all 11 observations because a killed invocation's final reading IS the limit it was terminated at. The operative boundary was "EVERY tick is over budget", not "a tick is over budget".
  - **An observation your model calls impossible is the most valuable one you have.** The 14 ms `ok` reading contradicted the ceiling a week early and was filed as a curiosity.
  - **Verify state against the authority the system actually reads,** not against a human proxy for it.
  - **An empty log array proves non-COMPLETION, not non-ENTRY,** when the only success log is the last statement of the handler.
  - **A wrong PASS CRITERION is as dangerous as a wrong hypothesis.** The pre-stated Stage 1 criterion predicted continued failure and would have FAILED on a healthy 76 ms startup reading (startup is a separate 1-second budget, not the invocation's 10 ms). Applied literally it would have declared a working fix broken. Criteria stated in advance guard against post-hoc rationalisation, but they inherit every assumption held when they were written — when a clearly better outcome contradicts one, re-examine the criterion before re-opening the diagnosis.
  - **Anchored extrapolations do not generalise across paths.** A ~1.5–2.3x desktop-to-platform factor derived from the IDLE path underestimated the live-path recovery by 2–4x.
