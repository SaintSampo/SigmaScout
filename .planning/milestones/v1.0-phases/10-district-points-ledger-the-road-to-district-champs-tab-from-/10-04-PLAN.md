The reason is the real mechanic: a top seed accepted as a higher alliance's FIRST PICK is removed

The progressive rule — at alliance `n`'s turn, the captain is the highest-ranked team not yet
allied — was checked teacher-forced against the real first picks: **3,879 of 3,880 captain slots
correct, 99.97%**, across **485 usable events** of 491, with **484 of 485 events perfect**. The

**Fact 2 — the draft order is serpentine, and the gradient proves it.** Mean qual rank of the real
SECOND pick, by alliance number, over all 491 events: alliance 1 = 24.83, 2 = 24.82, 3 = 23.64,

Tests before implementation, in two files.

**`packages/core/algorithms/simulation/continuousQuantile.test.ts`** — the promotion's own proof,
beyond the shipped `simQuantile.test.ts` which keeps running unchanged through the re-export.

- The promoted export and the `apps/web/src/lib/simQuantile.js` export are the SAME function object (strict identity, `toBe`), not two functions that agree. One implementation is the whole point of the promotion.
- A counts array and its normalised probability version give the same quantile: the estimator called on raw counts with the draw count equals the estimator called on counts divided by draws with a denominator of 1, to within 1e-12, for three different shapes and for p of 0.1, 0.5 and 0.9. This is what licenses passing a convolved pmf with a denominator of 1 in Task 3.
- An `Int32Array` and a plain array holding the same values return the identical value, so the `ArrayLike` contract holds for the typed arrays this plan produces.

**`packages/core/districts/ledgerSimulation.test.ts`** — pure, synthetic, no corpus, runs in CI.
Fixtures small enough that every expectation is hand-derivable: 24 teams (the minimum for eight
three-team alliances), a handful of remaining matches, ratings chosen so the SPR ordering is
unambiguous and distinct from the rank ordering.

- **THE CORRELATION PROOF (the load-bearing test).** Run the joint simulation with a per-draw observer, and assert that in EVERY draw the team the ranking placed first is a captain in that same draw's alliance selection — specifically the captain of alliance 1, since nothing can displace the top seed from the first captain slot. Then the stronger form: in every draw, every captain's rank is at or below the rank of every unallied team, which is the progressive rule's own invariant. Zero draws may violate either. A marginal comparison after the fact cannot detect a broken join here, which is why this assertion is per draw.
- **THE NON-PERTURBATION PIN.** The qualification marginal from the joint run is entry-for-entry identical (exact integer equality, not a tolerance) to the histogram a bare four-argument `simulateRanks` call produces under the same seed, pushed through `districtQualPoints(season, tier, rank, fieldSize)`. This proves three things at once: the hook consumed none of the rank stream, the qual push-through is the right formula on the right index, and the run is deterministic.
- **Determinism.** Two runs with the same seed produce byte-identical histograms for all five categories. Two runs with different seeds differ in at least one category. A run is reproducible across a round trip through `structuredClone` of the result.
- **The result is structured-clone-safe.** `structuredClone(result)` succeeds and the cloned histograms hold the same values; every map value is a typed array and no value anywhere in the result is a function.
- **Every histogram is a true marginal of the same runs.** For each category, the sum of the counts over one team's histogram equals `draws` exactly. And the per-draw quadruple sums to the event total on the same draw: with a per-draw observer capturing all five numbers for one team, the four category values sum to that draw's event total in every draw, and tallying those per-draw event totals reproduces the returned event-total histogram exactly.
- **Ceilings are read, not assumed.** Every histogram's length equals `maxEventPoints(season, tier)` for that category plus one, and the event-total length equals the sum of the four plus one.
