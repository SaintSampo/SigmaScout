ZERO of the four components earns its place. Notes on each, none of them
smoothed toward a nicer answer:

- C2a FOUL fails Rule A outright, and it is the only rung that does: its Brier
  gets WORSE (+0.00013, and that interval EXCLUDES zero, so the degradation is
  real rather than noise). It buys a little accuracy by making its probabilities
  worse. Rule A exists precisely to catch that trade and it catches it here.
  LADDER-RULE.md item 7's foulOn handicap does NOT apply - L2a started ABOVE L1
  (73.164 vs 73.086), so this failure is on the component's own merits with no
  structural excuse available to it.

- C2b ELIM passes Rule A but its interval includes zero and it is less than half
  the bar. See the cross-algorithm note in LADDER.md: this is the direction the
  standing VPR negative result predicted, arrived at independently on BPR.

- C2c DEFENSIVE is the near miss and it must be reported as a miss. +0.155pp
  against a 0.169pp bar - short by 0.014pp, about one part in twelve. It clears
  Rule A and Signal cleanly. It is the single most interesting failure on the
  ladder and the one most worth revisiting if the bar is ever re-derived on a
  larger design population - but the bar is a STANDING pre-existing constant and
  this task does not move it. Moving a threshold by 0.014pp to admit the
  component it was being applied to is the exact forking-paths move the
  predecessor named and refused, and refusing it here costs nothing that a
  future honest re-derivation could not recover.

- C2d HUBER is a calibration knob, as DECISION.md's ~0.00pp accuracy ablation
  said. Its Brier interval excludes zero in its favour, but the promotion rule
  is accuracy-gated and it is nowhere near the bar.

AN OBSERVATION THAT SUPPORTS THE EX-NO-CALL REASONING, obtained for free:

  In all four C2 contrasts the as-scored row and the ex-no-call row are
  IDENTICAL to the printed precision, and only ONE unit is dropped between them
  (n=82,735 -> 82,734). Both sides of these contrasts are built on L1, which has
  already broken the cold-start ties, so there is no no-call population left for
  the two numbers to disagree about.

  That is the whole ex-no-call argument, visible as a measurement rather than
  asserted: the as-scored/ex-no-call divergence is a property of the L0 baseline
  specifically, not a general property of the pairing. It appears ONLY where the
  incumbent is the frozen model with biasLr=0, and it vanishes the moment the
  incumbent can break a tie. A 2023 replay is warm-started and can always break
  a tie, so it is in the regime where the two numbers coincide.
