# Can we rank every team at an event by how likely they are to win each award?

5n8 and 7bp both asked "does the single top pick win?" and threw away everything else the model
computed. **The ranking already exists and is being discarded.** `pickByWeights`
(`scripts/measureAwardPredictability.ts:786`) computes `u = x·beta` for **every** team in the pool and
then hands the whole vector to `argmaxIndex` (`:766`), which returns one index and drops the rest.
This task keeps the vector, scores the whole ordering, and asks whether the stated probabilities are
honest.

The deliverable is a measurement and a plain-language answer. **No model, no surface, no artifact, no
publish.**

## Why this is the right next question

The chain's central finding is that on the flagship judged awards the fitted model **loses on top-1**
to B1, "pick the most decorated team in the room": Impact 22.1% vs 24.5%, Engineering Inspiration
9.6% vs 12.1%, Safety 22.8% vs 28.2% — and 7bp showed team age moves none of them.

But a 22% top-1 against a ~40-team pool is not the only useful shape. "The winner is in this list of
3" is a different and plausibly far more useful capability, and nobody has measured it. It is also
possible that the ordering below rank 1 is garbage and the top-1 number was the whole story. Both
outcomes are results; this task is built so either one can be stated honestly.

## The trap this plan exists to not repeat

7bp's load-bearing lesson: **a metric measured against a baseline that is structurally incapable of
scoring is not a measurement.** B1 and B2 are pinned at exactly 0.0% on the three rookie award types,
and 5n8's apparent +12 to +18pp model "wins" there were artifacts of beating a zero. RB1/RB2 shipped
in the same commit as the age feature specifically to kill that.

**The identical fallacy is available in rank form and is the default outcome if this plan is executed
carelessly.** A model ranking compared only against random would look spectacular everywhere. So:

> **LOAD-BEARING REQUIREMENT.** Every rank metric is reported for **B1 promoted to a full ordering**
> alongside the model, in the same table, on the same instances. A model ranking that is not compared
> against the decoration ranking is not a result. B2, RB1, RB2 and an exact-expectation random null
> are reported beside them. A plan output that shows model rank metrics without B1's is a failed
> execution whatever the numbers say.

### T1 — keep the ranking (executor, offline)

Extend `scripts/measureAwardPredictability.ts` and `scripts/measureAwardPredictability.test.ts`. One
commit.

**Every ordering is derived from the existing pick, never beside it.** This is the structural move
that makes the k=1 control real rather than asserted:

- `scoreByWeights(weights, features): number[]` — lift the dot-product loop out of `pickByWeights`
  verbatim. `pickByWeights` becomes `argmaxIndex(scoreByWeights(w, f))` and keeps its signature and
  its width-agnostic `weights.length` loop, so neither arm can read the other's width.
- `orderMostDecorated(candidates, awardType, history): number[]` — candidate indices sorted by the
  comparator `pickMostDecorated` already implements (prior wins of **this** type desc, prior wins of
  any type desc, team number asc), plus **candidate index asc** as the final key so the order is
  total even for two unparseable keys. `pickMostDecorated` becomes `orderMostDecorated(...)[0] ?? -1`.
  **It still takes no ratings argument** — the "silently becomes a two-feature model" mistake stays
  unavailable at the signature level.
- `orderStrongest(candidates, ratings): number[]` — same treatment of `pickStrongest`: pre-event BPR
  desc, unrated last (never a fabricated rating), team number asc, index asc. `pickStrongest` becomes
  its head.
- `orderMostDecoratedRookie` / `orderStrongestRookie` — the same comparators restricted to
  `knownRookieIndices` (`:895`). **The ordering has length |rookie block|, not |pool|**: no tail is
  invented for the non-rookies. The RB picks become the head of the block ordering, or `ABSTAIN` when
  the block is empty — reproducing today's behaviour exactly.

**Ranks.** `ranksFromOrder(order): Map<index, rank>` assigns 1-based ranks in ordering position. For
the model arms the order is `(u desc, candidate index asc)` — the **same** total order `argmaxIndex`'s
first-wins tie-break already implies, so `rank(i) === 1` if and only if `pickByWeights` returns `i`.
That identity is not a comment; T1 asserts it as a test.

**Winner rank for a multi-recipient award = the BEST (minimum) rank among the recipients that are in
the ordering.** Winner and Finalist carry 3-4 recipients, so "the winner's rank" is otherwise
ambiguous. This choice is the natural one and it is also the *generous* one: **recall@k is
mechanically inflated for multi-recipient awards** because more recipients means more chances to land
inside k. That is one more reason types 1 and 2 stay in the existing **REFERENCE-ONLY** section and do
not headline anything. Print the inflation warning in that section's header text, next to the
existing one.

**The two denominators, stated once and applied to all six predictors.** Getting this wrong is what
silently breaks the k=1 control:

| metric | denominator | instances with no reachable recipient |
|---|---|---|
| `recall@k`, k ∈ {1,3,5,10} | all scored instances (`n`) | score 0, stay in the denominator |
| MRR | all scored instances (`n`) | contribute reciprocal rank 0 |
| mean rank, median rank, normalized rank percentile | `rankDefined` only | excluded **and counted** |

`rankDefined` = instances where at least one recipient appears in that predictor's ordering. For the
model arms, B1 and B2 that is `n - unreachable`. For RB1/RB2 it is instances where the rookie block is
non-empty **and** contains a recipient; an empty block is the existing abstention and is already
counted in `rb1Abstentions`/`rb2Abstentions`.

Because recall@k keeps the full `n` and scores an unreachable or abstaining instance 0, **recall@1 is
by construction identical to today's top-1 rate for all six predictors.** That is the control.

**Normalized rank percentile** = `winnerRank / orderingLength`. Pools average ~40 but vary widely by
event, so a raw rank is not comparable across events and this is the form that is. For model/B1/B2 the
divisor is the pool size; **for RB1/RB2 it is the rookie-block size, which is a different denominator
and must be printed in its own column with the denominator named in the header** — it is not
comparable to the model's and the output must not let a reader assume it is.

**B0 — the random null ordering, computed exactly, never sampled.** No RNG, no seed, no run-to-run
drift. With `N` = pool size and `r` = recipients in the ordering, survival
`S(m) = Π_{j=0}^{r-1} (N−m−j)/(N−j)` (clamped at 0):

- `recall@k = 1 − S(k)` for `k ≤ N`, and 1 for `k ≥ N`
- `MRR = Σ_{m=1}^{N−r+1} (1/m)·(S(m−1) − S(m))`
- `mean rank = (N+1)/(r+1)`, the exact expectation of the minimum of `r` uniform draws from `N`
- `median rank` = the smallest `m` with `S(m) ≤ 0.5`
- normalized percentile = `meanRank / N`

B0 is the reference row, not a verdict participant.

**`Cell` gains one `RankStats` per predictor**, not a scatter of fields:

```
interface RankStats {
  rankDefined: number;
  recallAt: number[];        // parallel to K_VALUES
  reciprocalRankSum: number; // over all n; 0 for unreachable/abstained
  rankSum: number;           // over rankDefined only
  normalizedRankSum: number; // over rankDefined only
  ranks: number[];           // over rankDefined only, for the exact median
}
```

`export const K_VALUES = [1, 3, 5, 10] as const;`. The `--json` emitter **replaces `ranks` with the
computed median and its count** — otherwise the JSON carries ~366k integers and stops being readable.
Say why in the emitter, not in this plan only.

**Output.** The existing accuracy block per award type stays **byte-identical in shape** — same
`HEADER`, same columns, same per-season rows — so the control is legible at a glance. A new **RANK
block** follows it, **pooled only** (per-season rank rows would drown the output): one row per
predictor (`no-age`, `+age`, `B1`, `B2`, `RB1`, `RB2`, `B0`) with columns
`R@1 R@3 R@5 R@10 MRR meanRank medRank norm% rankDef`. Also print, per type, **the count of instances
whose pool is smaller than 10** — recall@10 on a 6-team pool is trivially 100% and the reader must be
able to see that.

**Walk-forward is untouched and the leak test must be extended to cover the new state.** Season Y's
fit and every prior count still come from seasons `< Y`. The new orderings are derived from the same
per-instance objects, so nothing new crosses the boundary — but "nothing new crosses" is a claim that
gets asserted, not assumed: extend both existing leak tests (`:225` feature-builder, `:647`
end-to-end) so the **rank metrics** for season Y are identical whether or not seasons `≥ Y` exist
beyond it, on a fixture where a leak would change them.

**Tests** (extending `scripts/measureAwardPredictability.test.ts`, synthetic fixtures, no corpus):

- **The k=1 control, for all six predictors:** `recallAt[0] === hits` on a shared fixture, for
  `model`, `ageModel`, `b1`, `b2`, `rb1`, `rb2`. An abstaining RB1 and an unreachable instance both
  land in the denominator and score 0 at every k.
- `rank(i) === 1` ⟺ `pickByWeights` returns `i`, including on a deliberate exact utility tie where
  first-wins must decide.
- `pickMostDecorated` and `pickStrongest` return exactly `order[0]` on fixtures covering each
  tie-break level, including two unparseable team keys where only the index tie-break separates them.
- `orderMostDecorated` never consults BPR — reshuffling the ratings map cannot move any position in
  its ordering (the signature-level guard, asserted behaviourally).
- The RB orderings contain only known-rookie indices and never a non-rookie, and their length is the
  block size.
- Multi-recipient winner rank is the **minimum** over recipients, not the first-listed one.
- B0's exact formulas match a **brute-force enumeration over all C(N, r) placements** on a small pool
  (e.g. N=6, r=2) for recall@k, MRR, mean rank and median rank. This is the test that proves the
  closed forms rather than trusting the algebra.
- The two-denominator rule: mean/median/normalized exclude unreachable instances while recall and MRR
  include them, asserted on a fixture where the two denominators differ.
- The extended leak tests.

### T2 — are the stated probabilities honest? (executor, offline)

Same two files, second commit. Depends on T1 and **must not run in parallel with it or in a separate
worktree** — both tasks edit the same two files in the same checkout.

The project's stated core value is "honest uncertainty". A ranking that cannot be trusted as a
probability is half an answer, so this is not optional polish.

**Probabilities.** Softmax of the utility vector over the pool, numerically stabilised by the same
`max` subtraction `fitConditionalLogit` (`:722-731`) already uses:
`p_i = exp(u_i − max) / Σ_j exp(u_j − max)`. It sums to 1 across the event by construction. Both arms
get their own, from their own weights.

**Two exclusions, each counted and printed, neither papered over:**

1. **Thin-prior rows have no probability.** Below `THIN_PRIOR_INSTANCES` neither arm is fit and the B1
   heuristic stands in (`:1367-1374`). A heuristic emits no probability, so those rows are excluded
   from every calibration number and counted. **Do not assign a fabricated probability.**
2. **Multi-recipient instances break the sum-to-1 premise.** A softmax models "exactly one winner";
   where `|recipients ∩ pool| > 1` there is more than one positive against a total mass of 1, which
   pushes observed rates *above* predicted and manufactures a fake under-confidence. The headline
   reliability table and Brier are therefore computed over **single-recipient-in-pool instances only**,
   with the excluded count printed. The multi-recipient set's mean predicted vs mean observed rate is
   printed as **one extra line** so it is visible rather than hidden.

**Reliability table — pooled across all judged award types, one table per arm.** Per-type would be too
thin to read. **Fixed bucket edges**, so the two arms and any future run stay comparable:
`[0, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05, 0.10, 0.20, 0.35, 0.50, 1.0]`. Uniform-width deciles would put ~97% of the mass
in one bucket, because with ~40 candidates the base rate is ~0.025. Each row prints: edges, **count of
candidate-slots**, mean predicted, observed win rate, and signed gap. **A bucket with fewer than 50
slots prints a `THIN` flag and no conclusion may be drawn from it** — state that in the table header,
not just in the SUMMARY.

**Brier.** Per candidate slot, `(p_i − y_i)²` with `y_i = 1` iff candidate `i` is a recipient; mean
over all slots in all included instances. **A raw multiclass Brier here is uninterpretable on its own**
— predicting `1/N` everywhere already scores ≈0.024 — so it never prints alone. Print beside it:

- **`BS_uniform`** — the same score for `p = 1/N`. This is exactly B0's Brier; unify them and say so.
- **Brier skill score `1 − BS_model / BS_uniform`.** This is the honest headline. Positive means the
  probabilities carry information beyond "everyone is equally likely"; ≤ 0 means they do not.

Both arms, pooled and per award type (one extra line in each type's block, not a new table).

**The single most legible calibration fact, required as its own printed line per award type:** the
mean predicted probability of the model's **top-1 pick** next to that pick's **observed hit rate**.
"The model says its top pick wins 31% of the time; it actually wins 22%" is the sentence a reader
understands immediately, and it is the one that says whether the number on a hypothetical page would
be a lie.

**Say the verdict in the output, before a human writes the SUMMARY.** The script prints one of
`PROBABILITIES USABLE AS STATED` / `PROBABILITIES NEED RECALIBRATION` per arm, from a rule
pre-committed here, before any number is seen:

> Usable iff the Brier skill score is positive **and** the top-1 stated-vs-observed gap is within
> ±5pp **and** no bucket with ≥ 50 slots is off by more than 10pp. Anything else is
> **NEEDS RECALIBRATION**. Not "roughly calibrated", not "directionally fine".

**Tests:**

- Softmax sums to 1 over the pool and is invariant to adding a constant to every utility (the
  stabiliser is correct, not just present).
- A thin-prior row contributes to **no** calibration accumulator and is counted in the exclusion.
- A multi-recipient instance is excluded from the headline table and counted.
- Brier on a hand-checkable fixture equals the hand-computed value; `BS_uniform` equals the analytic
  `(1 − 1/N)²/N + (N−1)/N³`-equivalent computed directly on the fixture; skill score is 0 when the
  model *is* uniform.
- Bucket assignment is closed-below/open-above at every edge, with the top bucket closed at 1.0, so no
  probability is dropped or double-counted — assert on probabilities sitting exactly on edges.
- The usable/needs-recalibration rule fires each way on fixtures built to sit either side of each of
  its three clauses.
