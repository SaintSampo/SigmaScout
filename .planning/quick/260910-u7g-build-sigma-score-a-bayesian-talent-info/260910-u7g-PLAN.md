# Quick task 260910-u7g — Sigma Score vs Swing Score

Experiment. **Nothing is wired into publish.** Follows quick task 260910-sz9, which measured the
shipped Swing Score and found it achieves Spearman 0.06–0.24 against a ~0.59 ceiling, fails to beat a
single population constant for EPA and BPR, and carries a near-zero tail that is catastrophically
overconfident.

## The requirement, in the developer's words

> The whole purpose is to tell a scout how consistent a robot is. How wildly might their performance
> swing next match? If a robot breaks, or finally starts working, Sigma Score captures that for a
> scout. VS if they have performed the same every match, Sigma Score should be low.

Two testable properties fall out, and they are evaluated explicitly (see "Regime-change test"):

1. **Responsiveness** — a robot whose performance level shifts must show a HIGH Sigma Score.
2. **Quietness** — a robot that performs identically every match must show a LOW Sigma Score.

**A design consequence worth stating up front:** requirement 1 constrains the centring. The shipped
Swing Score centres on a fast (half-life 6) running mean, so a level shift gets absorbed INTO the mean
within a few matches and stops registering as volatility — it hides exactly what the metric is for.
Mean-tracking half-life is therefore a knob under test, not an inherited constant.

## The estimator

Per team, deviation input is unchanged from Swing — `(actual − predicted) / rosterSize`, the even-split
share — so the comparison isolates the ESTIMATOR and not the input.

**Model.** `d = b_i(t) + e`, with `b_i(t)` the team's slowly-varying model bias and `e ~ N(0, σ_i²)`.
Sigma Score reports the posterior predictive spread of the next `d`.

**Conjugate Bayesian treatment of σ_i².** Inverse-gamma prior, normal likelihood:

```
prior:      σ² ~ InvGamma(α₀, β₀),  α₀ = priorObs/2,  β₀ = (priorObs/2)·priorSigma²
posterior:  α = α₀ + W/2,           β = β₀ + S/2
report:     SigmaScore = SIGMA_SCALE · √(β / (α − 1))        [posterior predictive SD]
```

`W` and `S` are the recency-weighted observation count and sum of squared residuals about `b_i(t)`.
`priorObs` is the shrinkage strength in pseudo-observations; `α > 1` requires `priorObs > 2`, enforced.

This structurally fixes the three measured defects: a two-observation team cannot reach σ≈0 because
`β ≥ β₀`, thin evidence is pulled toward the prior, and the predictive (not point) spread is what gets
reported so uncertainty about σ widens the answer rather than being discarded.

**The talent prior, walk-forward.** `priorSigma = priorK · max(talent, TALENT_FLOOR)`, where `talent`
is the team's own `TOTAL_METRIC_KEY` value read from the algorithm's state BEFORE the match
(`algorithm.teamMetrics(state, teamsInThisMatch)`), and `priorK` is a single running population
estimate `√(Σ residual² / Σ talent²)`. Stronger robots swing more in absolute points, so the prior
must scale with talent; `priorK` being one running number keeps it walk-forward and cheap.

**Scale.** Defaults to **1.0**, not Swing's 1.92 — Sigma Score is reported as an honest 1σ in points,
and coverage is measured to prove it. Swing's 1.92 was measured to deliver 88–91% coverage where a σ
label implies 68.3%.

## Scoring — identical rows, estimator-independent target

Every candidate supplies BOTH a location (its own bias estimate) and a scale, and is scored on the
**actual raw next deviation** — one target, shared by all, so no candidate's own centring choice can
define the thing it is graded against.

1. **Gaussian NLL** of the raw deviation under `N(location, scale²)`. Proper scoring rule; punishes
   over- and under-confidence both. **Median reported alongside mean** — 260910-sz9 established that a
   log score's mean is dominated by a handful of catastrophic rows.
2. **Spearman(scale, |raw dev|)** — fully common target, no location involved, so it ranks candidates
   on spread information alone.
3. **Coverage** `P(|raw dev − location| ≤ scale)`, against 68.3%.
4. **Mismatched-pairing control**, as in `measureSwingSkill.ts`, so an inflated correlation announces
   itself.

## Regime-change test — the developer's requirement, measured directly

Labelled with hindsight (legitimate: labels are for evaluation, never fed to an estimator).

- **A shift** at team T, match N: `|mean(next 3 devs) − mean(prior 3 devs)| ≥ 15 points`.
  **Responsiveness** = mean ratio of the metric over matches N+1..N+3 to its level over N−3..N.
  Higher is better; this is "a robot breaks or finally starts working".
- **A quiet run**: a team whose trailing 6 deviations have an actual spread in the bottom quintile.
  **Quietness** = the metric's mean on quiet runs, relative to its mean on the top-quintile (volatile)
  runs. A LOWER ratio means the metric separates steady robots from erratic ones.

A candidate that wins NLL but fails responsiveness has not met the requirement and must not be
recommended on score alone.
