# Quick Task 260910-25c: Adversarial statistical review of BPR

**The core is one good idea wrapped in a standard Kalman filter. The filter does
not believe its own variance — and the number the site actually shows users is
the one nothing corrects.**

Full review: `260910-25c-REVIEW.md`.

## The core, and what is actually load-bearing

BPR is a per-team scalar Kalman filter on alliance scores, expressed in
**scale-free units** (rating 1.0 = one third of a league-average alliance
score), with a probit link whose temperature is learned online.

The scale-free normalization is the one genuinely novel idea and it is correct —
it is why a single hyperparameter set transfers to a season whose scoring level
was never observed, and therefore why the model can be frozen at all. The
Bayesian machinery around it is textbook; "an uncertain team moves further" is
what *any* Kalman gain does by construction, not a BPR invention.

One real advantage is **under**-claimed: BPR's predictive variance is
match-specific, while `opr.ts` and `epa.ts` both use one season-wide score SD for
every match. That, not the Bayesian branding, is a defensible source of the Brier
lead.

## The headline finding

Running the promoted parameters over 2016-2022 (166,188 alliance-observations):

| quantity | measured | honest value |
|---|---|---|
| `sd(z)` of standardized innovations | **0.7062** | 1.000 |
| converged link temperature `tau` | **0.45 - 0.63**, every season | 1.000 |
| `corr(z_red, z_blue)` | **0.0111** | — |

The filter states a score variance about twice what it realizes, and the learned
`tau` then divides the margin sd by roughly two more to rescue the win
probability. I tested the benign explanation — that red and blue score errors are
correlated, so summing their variances legitimately overstates — and **it failed
(rho = 0.011)**. The independence assumption is fine; the variance level is not.

**`obsSd` is doing two contradictory jobs**: it sets the Kalman gain *and* the
stated uncertainty, and the tuner chose it for the former. `tau` repairs the
consequence in the win probability. Nothing repairs it in the published interval.

`innovations.ts`'s own comment argues this is harmless because "a Kalman gain is
a RATIO" — but `obsSd` inflates the denominator only, so it is precisely the knob
that breaks the invariance that argument relies on.

### This is a live product defect

`redScoreVarianceOwn` is a published artifact field
(`packages/harness/pageArtifacts.ts:388`) and the web app renders
`Math.sqrt(...)` of it as the displayed sd
(`apps/web/src/components/event/EventMatchTable.tsx:305`).

**BPR's displayed `X ± Y` is ~1.42x too wide**, on the site's premier algorithm,
against a stated project value of honest uncertainty. And it is not a constant
error — by strength quintile the interval is **1.8x too wide for weak alliances**
and about right for the strongest.

## The championship deficit, decomposed

`260908-vqr` found champs was BPR's only accuracy deficit and left the cause
open. The innovation bias locates it on design-era data, with a monotone gradient
in field strength (regional **+0.080** → district champs **-0.037** → champs
division **-0.232** → champs final **-0.409**; negative = over-predicts).

Regressing `z` on own *and* opponent predicted strength splits it in two:

- **Opponent coefficient is negative everywhere** (-0.149 overall) — unmodelled
  opponent suppression. This is the `defPriorVar` component `260909-3oe` measured
  at +0.155pp against a 0.169pp bar, now supported by an **independent
  instrument**.
- **Own coefficient flips sign at champs** (+0.065 outside, **-0.217** inside) —
  the winner's curse in a field selected on the very rating being used.

The champs arithmetic closes to within 0.05 sd.

## Two documentation claims the code contradicts

1. **"Anti-additivity" is super-additive, always.** The rank weights
   `(1, 0.7, 0.5)` renormalize to `(1.3636, 0.9545, 0.6818)` with the largest
   weight on the largest rating, so by Chebyshev's sum inequality the weighted sum
   is **≥ the plain sum for every alliance** — up to **1.23x** for a lopsided one.
   It is a spread amplifier, not the field-congestion suppressor the docstring
   describes. The renormalization line that flips the sign is documented only as
   preserving scale-freeness.

2. **There is no "slow talent".** With `qSlow = 0.00002` (the grid floor) against
   `qFast = 0.015`, **92.5%** of every rating update lands in the component that
   decays 10% per match and is **zeroed each season boundary**. Only **22.2%** of
   a season's evidence ever reaches the mean that carries forward. It is an annual
   re-baseline plus a 6.6-match memory, not talent-versus-form.

Also found: exact rating ties are broken by **driver station** (slot 1 absorbs
100% more Kalman credit than slot 3 on identical evidence); `seasonShrink = 1.0`
is pinned at its identity value and does nothing; two promoted values sit on
their grid boundaries; and `packages/bpr/model.ts:396` writes `this.year`, which
nothing reads.
