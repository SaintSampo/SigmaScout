# Adversarial review of BPR — the estimator, not the scoreboard

Two predecessors already audited BPR's *numbers*: `260908-vqr` asked whether
78.05% was honest (it is; the ~3pp margin was not) and `260909-3oe` asked what
each component buys (nothing lean earns its place). Neither asked whether the
model is *right*. That is this review.

Everything below is measured on 2016-2022. No holdout figure was read or
produced, no parameter was written, and no file under `packages/` was modified.

---

## 1. The core, stripped

Delete every optional component and BPR is four moving parts:

```
    rating          one scalar per team, in SCALE-FREE units:
                    r = 1.0 means "contributes one third of a
                    league-average alliance score"

    observation     alliance foul-adjusted score, divided by an
                    ONLINE running estimate of this season's mean
                    alliance score   ->   u = 3 * score / scale

    update          linear-Gaussian (Kalman) fold of u onto the three
                    teams, gain proportional to each team's posterior
                    variance:   k_i = w_i * p_i / (pv + obsSd^2)

    link            P(red) = Phi( d / (tau * sqrt(v)) ),
                    tau learned online by SGD on log loss
```

Six numbers per team (`muL, pL, muS, pS`, plus unshipped foul and defense
channels), one global `scale`, one global `tau`. That is the whole model.

**One idea in here is genuinely novel, and it is not the Bayesian part.** It is
the scale-free normalization. FRC's point scale moves by more than 3x across
seasons; OPR and EPA both live in points and must therefore be re-fit or
re-scaled per season. BPR divides the scale out *before* the filter sees the
observation and *again* out of the win probability, so a single hyperparameter
set transfers to a season whose scoring level was never observed. That is the
load-bearing structural claim, it is correct, and it is the reason the model can
be frozen at all.

The Kalman machinery around it is textbook. Variance-proportional credit
assignment ("an uncertain team moves further on the same evidence") is the
defining property of *any* Kalman filter, not a BPR invention — it is what the
gain formula does by construction.

**One further advantage is real and under-claimed.** BPR's predictive variance
`v = red.pv + blue.pv + 2*obsSd^2` is *match-specific*: an alliance carrying a
rookie gets a wider distribution. `opr.ts` and `epa.ts` both use one season-wide
alliance-score SD for every match in the season. That difference — not the
Bayesian branding — is a genuine and defensible source of BPR's Brier lead.

## 2. What the promoted parameters actually do (and it is not what the docstring says)

`frozen-params.json` ships 14 live keys. Reading them as a fitted object rather
than as prose produces two contradictions with the model's own documentation.

### 2.1 There is no "slow talent". There is an annual re-baseline and a 6.6-match memory.

The docstring sells a two-timescale story: "slow talent L (a robot's underlying
quality) plus fast form S". The promoted fit sets `qSlow = 0.00002` (**the floor
of its search grid**) against `qFast = 0.015` — a 750x ratio — and
`fastPriorVar = 0.25` against `priorVar = 0.1`, so the "fast" component starts
with 2.5x the prior variance of the "slow" one.

Simulating the filter's own variance recursions under exactly those parameters
(`bpr-steady-state.txt`):

| match | `pL` | `pS` | share of each update that DECAYS |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.09130 | 0.17335 | 65.5% |
| 10 | 0.05719 | 0.06997 | 55.0% |
| 40 | 0.02370 | 0.06305 | 72.7% |
| 160 | 0.00785 | 0.06253 | 88.8% |
| 400 | 0.00508 | 0.06244 | **92.5%** |

At steady state **92.5% of every rating update lands in the component that decays
by 10% per match** (half-life 6.6 matches), and `muS` is then **zeroed outright at
every season boundary**. Over a fresh 60-match season, only **22.2%** of the total
evidence the filter absorbs ever reaches the mean that carries forward.

So the two timescales are real but the labels are inverted in importance. The
model is: *a mostly-frozen per-team offset, re-opened for a short window each
season by `seasonVar = 0.03`, plus a ~6.6-match exponential form rating that is
discarded every winter.* Calling `muL` "a robot's underlying quality" oversells a
quantity that receives roughly a fifth of the evidence.

This matters beyond naming. The port's docstring credits "season carryover
+1.80pp" — the single largest component credit in the model. What is actually
carried is an early-season-anchored offset, not an accumulated talent estimate.

### 2.2 "Anti-additivity" is super-additive. Always.

The docstring's stated mechanism is suppression: "an FRC alliance shares one
field and a finite supply of game pieces, so three elite scorers should not add
linearly ... teams are ranked within their alliance and contribute with weights
(1, w2, w3)". With `w2=0.7, w3=0.5` that reads as down-weighting the 2nd and 3rd.

But the weights are then **renormalized to sum to 3**, giving
`(1.3636, 0.9545, 0.6818)`, and the largest weight is matched to the largest
rating. By Chebyshev's sum inequality that guarantees the weighted sum is
**greater than or equal to the plain sum for every alliance**, with equality only
when all three ratings are identical. Verified numerically (`weight-checks.txt`):

| alliance | plain sum | BPR weighted | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| (1.00, 1.00, 1.00) | 3.000 | 3.000 | 1.0000 |
| (1.20, 1.00, 0.80) | 3.000 | 3.136 | 1.0455 |
| (2.00, 1.00, 0.50) | 3.500 | 4.023 | **1.1494** |
| (3.00, 0.60, 0.40) | 4.000 | 4.936 | **1.2341** |

The component is not anti-additivity. It is a **spread amplifier**: it says a
star-plus-two-weak alliance outscores three mediocre robots of the same total
rating. That may well be true — it is an empirical claim the design era endorsed
at +0.53pp — but it is the opposite of the mechanism the code claims to
implement, and the renormalization line that flips the sign is documented as
merely preserving scale-freeness.

Three further properties of this component are undesirable on their own terms:

- **It is a step function of a noisy ordering.** Two teammates whose ratings
  differ by 1e-9 receive weights differing by 43%. Posterior team sd at steady
  state is ~0.26, so the within-alliance ranking of similar teams is mostly
  noise, and the model responds to it discontinuously. This also makes the
  likelihood non-differentiable in `w2`/`w3`, which is why the search has to be a
  grid and why ablation deltas on this axis are noisy.
- **Exact ties are broken by driver station.** `order` sorts by rating with an
  ascending-index tiebreak, so three teams at identical ratings — every alliance
  of three rookies, and every alliance on a cold-start opening match — give slot 1
  **100% more Kalman credit than slot 3** on identical evidence.
- **It inflates the stated variance.** `pv = sum(w_i^2 * p_i)` and
  `sum(w_i^2) = 3.2355` against 3.0 additive, so an equal-strength alliance's
  predictive variance is 7.9% wider purely from the weighting. `tau` then partly
  absorbs that, entangling the component's measured credit with the link.

## 3. The filter does not believe its own variance

This is the review's main result, and it is new.

Run the promoted parameters over the design era and form the standardized
innovation `z = (observed - predicted) / sqrt(stated variance)`. If the filter's
variance were honest, `sd(z) = 1` and the learned `tau` would sit at 1.0.

```
  all alliance-observations   n=166,188   mean z  0.0379 (+-0.0034)   sd z 0.7062

  converged link temperature tau
    end of 2016  0.4595      end of 2019  0.4672
    end of 2017  0.6264      end of 2020  0.6206
    end of 2018  0.4508      end of 2022  0.5417
```

**`sd(z) = 0.706`, and `tau` never once approaches 1.0.** The filter states a
score variance twice the size of the one it realizes, and then a second, learned
knob divides the margin sd by roughly a further factor of two to rescue the win
probability.

I tested the obvious benign explanation and it failed. If red's and blue's score
errors were positively correlated (shared field, pace, refereeing), the summed
margin variance would legitimately overstate. Measured directly:

```
  corr(z_red, z_blue) = 0.0111        [the model assumes 0]
  implied honest margin variance = 0.989x the variance BPR states
```

Essentially zero. The model's independence assumption is *fine*; the variance
level is not.

### Why this is a structural defect, not a cosmetic one

`obsSd = 1.0` is doing two jobs that pull in opposite directions. It sets the
**Kalman gain** (`k = w*p/(pv + obsSd^2)`, so a larger `obsSd` learns more slowly
and produces more conservative ratings) and it sets the **stated uncertainty**.
The tuner selected it for winner accuracy, i.e. for its learning-rate role. `tau`
then silently repairs the resulting probability.

The comment in `innovations.ts` argues a global level error is harmless because
"a Kalman gain is `P / (pv + obsVar)`, a RATIO. Inflating numerator and
denominator together leaves learning untouched." That is true of a common
rescale — but `obsSd` inflates the **denominator only**. It is precisely the knob
that breaks the ratio invariance the comment relies on.

So BPR has two knobs governing three quantities: learning rate, stated
uncertainty, and probability sharpness. `tau` absorbs the mismatch **in the win
probability**, which is why accuracy and Brier look excellent. Nothing absorbs it
in the published interval.

### The consequence is user-facing

`predict()` emits `redScoreVarianceOwn = (red.pv + obsSd^2) * unit^2` — exactly
the quantity whose `sd(z)` was just measured at 0.706. It is a published artifact
field (`packages/harness/pageArtifacts.ts:388`) and the web app renders
`Math.sqrt(redScoreVarianceOwn)` directly as the displayed sd
(`apps/web/src/components/event/EventMatchTable.tsx:305`).

**BPR's displayed `X ± Y` is about 1.42x too wide**, on the algorithm that is now
the site's premier model, against a stated project value of "honest uncertainty".

And the error is not a constant. Binning `sd(z)` by predicted alliance strength
on 2022 (`innovations.ts`, all weeks):

| quintile | mean mu | sd z | interval is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 | 1.706 | 0.5507 | **1.8x too wide** |
| Q2 | 2.429 | 0.6809 | 1.5x too wide |
| Q3 | 2.946 | 0.7409 | 1.3x too wide |
| Q4 | 3.499 | 0.8020 | 1.2x too wide |
| Q5 | 4.559 | 1.0011 | about right |

A single scalar cannot fix that, which is exactly what the shelved `obsSdSlope`
diagnostic said — and this reproduces it on the **design era**, so unlike the
2023 wk0-1 diagnostic that motivated it, this evidence spends no holdout.

## 4. The championship deficit has two named mechanisms, both measurable, both absent from the model

`260908-vqr` found BPR's only measured accuracy deficit at championships (74.67
vs EPA 76.51) and left the cause open. The innovation bias locates it, on
design-era data, with a clean monotone gradient in field strength:

```
  BY EVENT TYPE   (mean z < 0 = the model OVER-predicts alliance output)
    regional / district (0,1)     n=137,666   mean z  +0.0804 (+-0.0038)
    district championship (2)     n= 10,030   mean z  -0.0366 (+-0.0125)
    CHAMPIONSHIP division (3)     n= 13,268   mean z  -0.2320 (+-0.0102)
    CHAMPIONSHIP final (4)        n=    280   mean z  -0.4091 (+-0.0640)

  BY COMP LEVEL
    qualification                 n=137,480   mean z  +0.0551 (+-0.0036)
    elimination                   n= 28,708   mean z  -0.0442 (+-0.0089)
```

Regressing `z` jointly on the alliance's **own** predicted strength and its
**opponent's** predicted strength separates two candidate causes:

| population | intercept | OWN strength | OPPONENT strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| all design-era (n=166,188) | 0.3499 | **+0.0434** (±0.0048) | **-0.1488** (±0.0048) |
| non-championship (n=152,640) | 0.2745 | **+0.0654** (±0.0049) | **-0.1379** (±0.0049) |
| championship (n=13,548) | 1.1569 | **-0.2168** (±0.0175) | **-0.2064** (±0.0175) |

*(Naive SEs. Observations cluster by match and event, so these intervals are
optimistic — but the effects are an order of magnitude larger than the noise.)*

Two distinct, correctly-signed misspecifications:

1. **Unmodelled opponent suppression.** The opponent coefficient is negative
   *everywhere*, at every event tier: a stronger opponent measurably reduces your
   output, and the shipped model has no term for it. This is exactly the
   `defPriorVar`/`defQ` component that `260909-3oe` measured at +0.155pp and
   failed the 0.169pp bar by 0.014pp. It is now supported by an **independent
   instrument** — a mean-innovation regression on 166k observations rather than an
   accuracy delta — pointing the same direction.

2. **Selection bias in a selected population.** The own-strength coefficient
   *flips sign* at championships: **+0.065** outside, **-0.217** inside. Outside
   champs the ratings are over-shrunk (strong alliances beat their rating —
   consistent with `tau < 1` and a too-large `obsSd`). At championships, where the
   field is *selected on the very rating being used*, the highest-rated alliances
   systematically under-perform. That is the winner's curse, and no amount of
   extra components fixes it — it needs the ratings' own uncertainty handled
   correctly, which §3 shows is not currently the case.

The champs arithmetic closes: `1.157 - 0.206*3.4 - 0.217*3.4 ≈ -0.28` against a
measured `-0.236`.

Worth noting: the spread-amplification of §2.2 pushes the *other* way at
championships (strong fields are relatively less spread), so it is not the cause.

## 5. Critical vs fluff

**Already correctly cut.** The production port carries 14 of the research model's
23 knobs. The foul-conceded submodel, `elimWeight`, defensive suppression, Huber
clipping, learned side bias and `obsSdSlope` are genuinely *absent*, not
present-but-disabled. That is the right call and it is already done — credit
where due.

Of the 14 that shipped:

| part | verdict | why |
|---|---|---|
| scale-free normalization + online `scale` | **CRITICAL** | the one novel idea; the reason the model can be frozen across seasons at all |
| Kalman variance-proportional credit | **CRITICAL** | it is the estimator |
| season carryover (`seasonVar`) | **CRITICAL** | largest single credit (+1.80pp), though what carries is an offset, not "talent" (§2.1) |
| match-specific predictive variance | **CRITICAL, under-claimed** | the real source of the Brier lead; OPR/EPA use one season-wide SD |
| learned `tau` | **CRITICAL but a symptom** | it is load-bearing *because* the variance is wrong (§3). Fix the variance and it should approach 1 |
| two-timescale `L`/`S` | **mechanism real, names fluff** | it is a two-exponential memory kernel, not talent-vs-form (§2.1) |
| `w2` / `w3` rank weights | **suspect** | works, but the mechanism is mis-stated, non-differentiable, tie-broken by driver station, and variance-inflating (§2.2) |
| `seasonShrink = 1.0` | **DEAD** | promoted at its identity value; the "shrink toward 1.0" never fires. Also the **maximum** of its grid |
| `tau0 = 1.0` | **not a knob** | an initializer |
| `qSlow = 0.00002` | **at the grid floor** | the search wanted lower and could not go there; read it as "≈0", not as a fitted value |

Two grid-boundary hits (`qSlow` at min, `seasonShrink` at max) mean two promoted
values sit on the edge of the searched region and should not be quoted as optima.

**Dead code:** `packages/bpr/model.ts:396` assigns `this.year` and nothing ever
reads it. Write-only state.

**A note on the selection procedure.** `tune.ts` is greedy coordinate descent
accepting any accuracy gain above `ACC_NOISE = 0.0005` (0.05pp), across ~330
evaluations, when the design-era SE on accuracy is ~0.17pp and `260908-vqr`
measured a 1.45x design effect on top of that (honest bar ~0.25pp). Accepting at
one-fifth of a standard error, 330 times, reliably fits noise. This is the
mechanism behind the design-to-holdout shrinkage `260908-vqr` documented
(+3.05pp → +1.79pp) — that gap is textbook selection optimism, not a surprise.
The log-loss tiebreak mitigates it, since log loss is far lower-variance than
accuracy, but the accuracy-first rule at 0.05pp is the leak.

## 6. What to do, ranked

### A. Fix the published interval — free, prediction-neutral, no holdout cost

The displayed sd is ~1.42x too wide overall and ~1.8x too wide for weak
alliances. Apply a **display-time** calibration to `redScoreVarianceOwn` /
`blueScoreVarianceOwn` — a level factor plus a strength slope, fitted on
2016-2022 innovations.

This is the rare change that is **provably prediction-neutral**: it touches only
the emitted variance field, never `predict()`'s `pRedWin` and never any Kalman
gain. The sealed 78.05% is a statement about winner accuracy and is untouched.
It is a display bug fix, not a model change, and it needs no re-tune.

*Recommended. Nothing else here is this cheap or this clearly right.*

### B. Correct the two docstrings — free

`packages/core/algorithms/bpr.ts` states a sub-additive mechanism the code does
not implement (§2.2) and a talent/form decomposition the fit does not produce
(§2.1). This project's failure log names "the README described a model that had
been deleted" as a founding failure mode; this is the same shape, one notch
milder. Also delete the write-only `this.year`.

*Recommended.*

### C. Retire `seasonShrink`, and label `qSlow` honestly — free

`seasonShrink` is pinned at its identity value, so removing it changes no
prediction whatsoever and removes a knob from the model's stated complexity.
`qSlow` should be documented as sitting on the grid floor rather than quoted as a
fitted optimum.

*Recommended, if only to make the parameter count honest.*

### D. Opponent suppression and the smooth spread weight — **blocked by standing policy**

The evidence in §4 makes a strong mechanistic case for an opponent-suppression
term, and §2.2 makes a case for replacing the two rank-step weights with a single
smooth function of within-alliance spread (2 knobs → 1, differentiable, no
driver-station artifact, and it would make the docstring true).

**Both require re-tuning and a promotion decision, and BPR tuning is closed by
your 2026-09-09 decision.** I am recording the mechanisms because they are newly
measured and would otherwise be lost — not re-opening the question. If that
decision is ever revisited, §4's regression is the pre-existing evidence, and
`260909-3oe`'s L2c (defensive, +0.155pp, missed the bar by 0.014pp) is the
candidate it points at.

## 7. What I could not settle

- **Why `tau ≈ 0.50` when `sd(z) = 0.706` implies `tau ≈ 0.706`.** Within-match
  correlation is ruled out (0.011). The remaining candidates are non-Gaussian
  margin tails, under-dispersed point predictions (consistent with the +0.065
  own-strength coefficient outside champs), and `tau`'s SGD not reaching the MLE.
  Distinguishing them needs a likelihood profile over `tau`, which I did not run.
- **Whether §4's coefficients survive event-clustered inference.** The SEs quoted
  are naive. The effects are 10-40x their naive SEs, so the sign is not in doubt,
  but the magnitudes are not intervals I would publish.
- **Whether the champs selection effect is separable from the champs opponent
  effect.** Both regressors are large and correlated inside that population.
