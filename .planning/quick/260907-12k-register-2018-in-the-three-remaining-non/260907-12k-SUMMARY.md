# Quick task 260907-12k: Register 2018 in the three non-visible registries — Summary

2018 is now registered in `pointModel.ts`, `districtNames.ts` and `statbotics.ts`, closing rows
4, 5, 6 and 8 of the nine-registry table. Every value was re-verified against the local corpus or
live-fetched data rather than copied from 2019. **`FIRST_SEASON` remains 2019** — 2018 is
deliberately still invisible on the site.

## Why nothing here is user-visible, and why that was the point

Flipping `FIRST_SEASON` would put 2018 in the live year dropdown immediately, with **no 2018
artifacts in R2 behind it** — every 2018 page would 404. This project already lived that during
the 2019/2020 rollout, when a concurrent push deployed a year dropdown ahead of its artifacts.
The order is publish-then-reveal. All three registries landed here are inert until either a
publish or a harness run actually reaches 2018, which is exactly what made them safe to land now
while the publish decision is still open.

## District ceilings — re-measured, not copied

Query run against `data/corpus.sqlite` (1,543 `district_rankings` team rows, 3,724 `event_points`
entries) BEFORE any edit:

```
district: qual 22, alliance 16, elim 30, award 15   |   dcmp: qual 66, alliance 48, elim 90, award 30
rookie bonus max: 10
```

Identical to every other registered season, and the dcmp figures are exactly 3x their district
counterparts, confirming the weight of 3 directly. The re-measurement mattered even though the
numbers matched: `pointModel.ts`'s discipline is that a row copied from 2019 on the assumption
nothing changed is not a verified row.

One nuance recorded rather than smoothed over: 2018's *observed* dcmp award maximum is 30 where
2019's is 45. 2018 simply never had a team collect the full award ceiling at a DCMP. `award: 15`
is still correct as a ceiling — too high only delays a verdict, too low produces wrong lock math.

## The silent-trap pattern showed up a third time

`districtNames.test.ts` carried **no** closed-key-set assertion — only an iteration list over
`OBSERVED_2019_2020_DISTRICT_KEYS`. Adding `mar` and `nc` would have failed nothing and shipped
two unproven entries with a green suite. This is the same shape quick task 260906-8kd closed in
the breakdown suite and that 260907-057 found still live in `rules.test.ts` — **three files now,
in three different packages.**

The planner caught it and had the executor add a 2018 key-set test, then prove it bites.
Red-then-green was run for real, not asserted: with `mar` removed, two tests failed naming `mar`
directly (`expected 'MAR' to be 'FIRST Mid-Atlantic'`); restored, 4/4 green. The removed state was
never committed.

## Statbotics — 2018 exists, and corroborates the additivity finding

Added 2018 (`0.7435` / `0.1747`) and 2019 (`0.7322` / `0.1763`) fallback rows, both dated
2026-09-07. **2020 was deliberately not added** — nobody measured it, and the executor has no
network, so inventing it was the only alternative to leaving a named gap. It is recorded as a gap
needing one live GET.

Two corrections landed with it:
- An earlier orchestrator probe of `/v3/season/2018` returned 404 and briefly looked like "2018 is
  unavailable." That path is simply wrong; the endpoint is `/v3/year/{season}` and 2018 returns
  200. The file records this so nobody re-derives the false conclusion.
- `statbotics.ts:194`'s no-fallback error message hardcoded `(covered: 2022-2026)`, which these
  rows made false. It now derives its coverage list from `Object.keys(STATBOTICS_REFERENCE_FALLBACK)`.

Worth keeping: Statbotics' own 2018 accuracy (0.7435) sits well below its 2022 (0.7815).
Third-party corroboration that 2018 is genuinely harder to predict, independent of this project's
own measurement that it is the only anti-correlated season.
