# Soft credit allocation (`softCredit`)

BPR splits an alliance's surprise across its three robots by RANK. With the
shipped `w2 = 0.7 / w3 = 0.5` the renormalized multipliers are **1.364 / 0.955 /
0.682** — the top-ranked robot's rating counts exactly **double** the third's —
and that assignment is a step function of an ordering the model only knows to
within its own posterior.

## The defect, measured

`experiments/phase-bpr/knife-edge.ts` reads the real `BprModel`'s own pre-match
state (TypeScript `private` is compile-time only), so these are the shipped
model's own numbers, not a replica's.

| season | alliances | mean P(flip) 1v2 | 2v3 | ≥1 pair at P>0.40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | 32,580 | 0.279 | 0.297 | 49.5% |
| 2024 | 33,916 | 0.267 | 0.287 | 47.1% |
| 2025 | 35,630 | 0.264 | 0.286 | 46.8% |

**About half of all alliances contain a rank boundary that is effectively a coin
flip**, and a 43% credit difference is assigned across it anyway.

In qualifications a coin-flip ordering lands each way and averages out. In
**playoffs the alliance is fixed**, so the arbitrary ordering locks and repeats
for the whole run, self-reinforcing because whoever ranks first absorbs more of
every innovation.

Pairs co-appearing in ≥4 playoff matches at one event, bucketed by separability
at the START of the run:

| start separability | pairs | lock % | divergence | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| coin flip (P>0.40) | 1,029 | **91.2%** | **+0.0054** | **[+0.0020, +0.0087]** |
| unresolved (0.25–0.40) | 1,561 | 99.9% | +0.0021 | [−0.0006, +0.0047] |
| leaning (0.10–0.25) | 1,523 | 100.0% | −0.0117 | [−0.0147, −0.0089] |
| clear (P<0.10) | 2,521 | 100.0% | −0.0485 | [−0.0510, −0.0459] |

**The sign inversion isolates the mechanism.** The negative rows are ordinary
regression to the mean. That force is *strongest* for coin-flip pairs, where the
observed gap is purest noise — so they should be the most negative row of all.
Instead they are the only positive one. Something overcomes the regression that
should be happening, and the locked credit weight is the only candidate.

## The change

Each robot's CREDIT weight is `base` interpolated at its expected rank
`1 + Σ_j P(r_j > r_i)`. Confident orderings reproduce the hard assignment
exactly; unresolvable ones fall back toward equal credit.

After the change, the same measurement against the real shipped model:

| | coin-flip divergence | 95% CI |
|---|---|---|
| before | +0.0054 | [+0.0020, +0.0087] |
| **after** | **−0.0002** | **[−0.0030, +0.0025]** |

**Prediction still uses hard rank weights.** Softening it too was measured and
was worse: it partly disables the anti-additivity that earned promotion, costing
published-rating reliability at intervals clear of zero
(−0.0045 / −0.0035 / −0.0033 across 2023–2025), where softening credit alone
costs −0.0032 / −0.0029 / −0.0018 with every interval spanning zero.

## Not an accuracy claim

Winner accuracy moved **+0.014pp** and Brier **+0.00012** over 2023–2025. Both
are noise. The justification is correctness: a 43% credit difference should not
turn on a distinction the evidence cannot make.

The reliability cost is **not established**, but its point estimate is negative
in all three seasons. This is a good trade, not a free one.

## Two consequences worth stating

**No longer a strictly consistent Kalman update.** The gain's H (soft) no longer
matches the mean's H (hard). Deliberate approximation, defensible exactly where
applied — the hard ordering is wrong about a quarter of the time, so the "exact"
gain is exact with respect to an ordering the model does not believe.

**`BPR_VERSION` 2.0.0 → 3.0.0.** Covers this change *and* commit `7c88e234`
(adjustPoints), which altered every prediction but left the version at 2.0.0 —
under D-13 that would leave one artifact key standing for two structurally
different outputs.
