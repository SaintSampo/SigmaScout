## The mechanism

`updateAllianceSum` attributes a SHARED alliance-sum observation across teammates strictly by
variance share, `K_j = P_j / (Sum P_i + R)`. A green team among two veterans therefore absorbs
roughly half the innovation, on an assumption about who was responsible that `covariance.ts`'s
own header already flags as unrecoverable from a summed observation. `260906-8ao` Finding 4
established that the residual deficit is VARIANCE in exactly that thin-information regime, and
not bias.

This shrinks the attribution VECTOR toward uniform:

```
share_j   = P_j / (Sum P_i + R)
uniform_j = (Sum P_i / (Sum P_i + R)) / n
K_j       = (1 - lambda) * share_j + lambda * uniform_j
```

**`Sum_j K_j` is IDENTICAL for every lambda**, because `Sum_j share_j == Sum_j uniform_j ==
Sum P_i / (Sum P_i + R)`. The alliance's total learning per observation does not change. This is
NOT a slower filter — it only redistributes learning, trading a little attribution bias for
variance reduction.

That property is load-bearing, and the data confirms it. 260906-7fj's gain cap genuinely slows
the filter and cost 2024 **-0.57 SE**; attribution shrinkage at the comparable pooled gain cost
2024 only **-0.11 SE**, because 2024's need for fast adaptation is preserved by construction.
Shrinking the vector is strictly better than clipping its peak.

Concretely, for a green team among two veterans at lambda 0.30: its gain falls from ~0.50 to
~0.41, while each veteran's rises from ~0.03 to ~0.07. Same total absorbed, spread less extremely.

## The two mechanisms compose

They do different things and neither subsumes the other:

- **Attribution shrinkage** redistributes learning across teammates, `Sum_j K_j` unchanged.
- **Gain cap** reduces `Sum_j K_j` for alliances containing a dominant-variance team.

Alone: cap +0.52 SE, l30 +0.83 SE. Combined: **+1.14 SE** — sub-additive but clearly better than
either, and the combination is better than l70 ON 2024 specifically (-0.44 vs -0.78) while
matching it elsewhere.

Order matters and is fixed in `patch-combo.cjs`: shrink first, then clip. Capping first would let
the uniform blend lift a clipped gain back above the cap, so the cap would not bind.
