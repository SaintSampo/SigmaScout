# Quick task 260912-5n8: can FRC awards be predicted?

**Yes, well above chance — but essentially all of the signal is "this team has won this
award before", and a fitted model does not beat that heuristic on the awards people care
about most.**

## What was built

Two commits.

`384fc1b1` — the wide award ingest. `event_awards_all` (additive; `event_awards` untouched),
`packages/ingest/awardsAll.ts`, and a `--awards-all-only` CLI mode exposed as
`pnpm ingest:awards-all`. It fetches the same `/event/{key}/awards` endpoint the narrow mode
uses, but over **every** event type and keeping **every** award type and recipient, plus the
`name` and `awardee` fields the narrow table discards.

`81a2bdcb` — `scripts/measureAwardPredictability.ts` (`pnpm measure:award-predictability`,
~29s). Its own chronological replay mirrors `evaluate.ts`'s predict-before-update sequencing
(`runEval` itself is untouched) and snapshots `BprModel.snapshot()` at each event's first
match, so the strength feature is strictly pre-event. Per-award-type conditional logit, fit
walk-forward: season Y's coefficients and every prior-award count come from seasons `< Y` only.

## The backfill

10 seasons (2016-2020, 2022-2026; no 2021). **41,869 award rows across 28-33 distinct award
types per season**, against the 4 types the corpus held before. Roster coverage went from
patchy (only 2018 complete) to **100% of official events in all ten seasons**, which the
candidate pool depends on. 3,868 TBA requests total at the existing 100ms throttle; the
throttle was not touched.

## The answer

30,516 instances scored across 9 seasons (2017-2026; 2016 is prior-only). Events average
~39-41 candidate teams, so **random is 2.5-3%**.

## The finding that matters

**On the flagship judged awards the fitted model LOSES to the one-line heuristic.** Impact
22.1% vs 24.5%. Engineering Inspiration 9.6% vs 12.1%. Safety 22.8% vs 28.2%.
Entrepreneurship 14.9% vs 17.5%. Adding a pre-event BPR rating and a fitted conditional
logit buys nothing over **"pick the most decorated team in the room"**.

Only two award types show the model genuinely ahead, both by ~1.2pp: Excellence in
Engineering (14.3 vs 12.1) and Autonomous (18.9 vs 14.3). Autonomous is the one place the
strength feature earns its keep — BPR alone scores 17.7% there, far above its ~5% on the
outreach awards, which is what you would expect from an award that is actually about the robot.

## Three results that are NOT results, stated so they are not misread later

1. **Rookie All Star (10), Rookie Inspiration (15) and Highest Rookie Seed (14) show large
   apparent wins that are artifacts.** Both baselines are *structurally* pinned at 0.0% — the
   most-decorated baseline cannot pick a team with no prior wins, and the strength baseline
   cannot pick a team with no rating. The fit beats them by isolating the all-zero feature
   vector only a never-played, never-decorated team can hold: an implicit age detector
   assembled from the *absence* of the two allowed features. No third feature was added; the
   handicap is reported as found.

2. **Four PREDICTABLE verdicts are sub-1pp and are optimizer noise** — Team Spirit (+0.1pp),
   Quality (+0.2pp), Woodie Flowers Finalist (+0.6pp). Raising gradient iterations from 200 to
   1500 moves pooled accuracy by at most 0.6pp, which is enough to flip any verdict inside that
   band in either direction. The script prints the margin and tags these itself.

3. **The plan's premise about person-only awards was mechanically wrong.** TBA attaches a
   `team_key` to Woodie Flowers (3) and Dean's List (4), so the `team_key IS NULL` rule does not
   drop them — only 665 of 41,869 rows are truly person-only. Both sit in the judged section.
