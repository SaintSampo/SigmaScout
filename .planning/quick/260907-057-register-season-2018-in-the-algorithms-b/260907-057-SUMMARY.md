## The orchestrator got the scope wrong, and this records it

The brief declared `apps/web/**` entirely out of scope, reasoning that surfacing a season on the
website is a separate follow-up (as it was for 2019/2020). That was **wrong in one specific
place**, and it put two failing tests on `main`.

`apps/web/src/lib/bonusRp.ts`'s table is pinned against core's `RP_RULE_MODULES` season by season
by `bonusRp.test.ts`. Registering a season in core therefore *requires* the matching client entry
in the same change — it is not "surfacing a season on the website" (season list, districts,
publishing), it is the mechanical other half of registering an RP rule module. The 2019/2020
precedent (`ad70f6ef`) had already got this right by patching both together; the brief overlooked
it.

The executor followed the constraint exactly, left the suite red rather than working around it,
diagnosed the cause precisely, and flagged it — which is the correct behaviour under a wrong
instruction. The orchestrator then reversed the constraint and landed `b698e299`. A comment now
sits at the table explaining why it belongs with the module, so the 2017 and 2016 passes do not
repeat it.

**Lesson for the next two seasons:** the module/web split is real, but `bonusRp.ts` sits on the
module side of it.

## Why the tolerance is not a rubber stamp

Three independent binds, all asserted rather than declared: the measured `rate` cap, a
`maxAbsGap` cap on any single tolerated exception, and a `direction` check that fails on a
wrong-signed exception. `breakdownToleranceFor` returns `undefined` for every season without an
entry, so the mechanism is **inert by construction** for 2019–2026 — the "inert at default"
property this project requires of new knobs.

The executor ran a negative control: forcing `rate: 0` turned the suite red with
`measured rate 0.004 exceeds the named tolerance 0` (and 0.003 for blue), then restoring `0.008`
returned it green; only the green state was committed. The orchestrator did not re-run this, but
cross-checked it: the quoted failure message matches the assertion's own template at
`reconciliation.test.ts:203`, and the 0.004/0.003 rates match the sampled-window figures reported
independently elsewhere in the run. Those two agreeing is what makes the control credible.

The residual is TBA's own arithmetic, not a mapping defect — proven three ways and recorded in
`breakdown/2018.ts`'s header: `autoPoints == autoRun + autoOwnership` (0 mismatches),
`teleopPoints == teleopOwnership + vault + endgame` (0 mismatches), and all 196 exceptions also
fail TBA's *own* `totalPoints == autoPoints + teleopPoints + foulPoints + adjustPoints`.

  quietly, with the justification that a conservative branch would understate a bonus with a ~49%
  base rate entirely.
