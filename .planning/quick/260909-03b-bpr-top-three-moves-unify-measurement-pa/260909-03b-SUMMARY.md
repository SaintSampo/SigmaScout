## P3 — the honest design-era number

**73.081% → 72.871%, a drop of 0.210pp.** This is the honest number replacing a
flattering one, not a regression. Nothing about the model changed; only the
scoring did.

Two defects were retired:

- **The accuracy convention.** BPR's private rule awarded **half credit** for an
  unopinionated `pRed === 0.5` prediction against a decided match. Every other
  algorithm is scored by `core/scoring/brier.ts`'s `accuracyCall`, where D-Q3
  counts that a miss. BPR made 270 such no-calls in the design era, 269 of them
  in 2016.
- **The population.** BPR's private SQL dropped `event_type = 100` and any
  null-score row, and never applied the D-07 surrogate exclusion. It was scored
  on a population no other algorithm saw, so its published numbers were not
  comparable to anything.

The population now reconciles **exactly** against the review's shared-harness
recount — **0 mismatches across all ten seasons**, design-era denominator 81,790
matching to the row. Full table in `POPULATION-RECONCILE.txt`.

The pre-change value is recorded beside the new one in `BASELINE-PRE-P3.txt`,
in `equivalence.ts`'s `KNOWN_DESIGN` comment, and here — never silently replaced.

Two live holdout-read paths were closed: `equivalence.ts` printed a 2023–2026
figure on **every** invocation (now halts after 2022 unless `--include-holdout`),
and `slice.ts` defaulted its year argument to **2023** with no guard (now 2022,
requiring `--allow-holdout`).

The seal was extended from the parameter file alone to **six paths** — model,
evaluator, data loader, driver, shipped port, plus the named parameter files —
each printed with its blob sha (F-15: `model.ts` had changed twice after the
sealed holdout ran, unchecked). `--years` now binds the halt-after season to the
maximum year named, so a 2023 run **structurally cannot** step a 2024 match.
Four refusal/resolution transcripts in `GUARDS-PROOF.txt`.

Port fidelity, re-measured on the shared path: **+0.001pp** (72.871 research vs
72.871 port).

## P2 — the bar was wrong, and in the direction that costs you improvements

The review estimated the bar at "really ~0.45pp" from **marginal** standard
errors and correctly declined to conclude from it. Ablation deltas are **paired**
— both variants score the *same* matches, so shared match difficulty cancels
inside the difference before resampling.

| quantity | value |
|---|---|
| design effect (clustered ÷ naive binomial) | **1.430x** (review: 1.45x — reproduced) |
| **level** two-sigma bar | **0.445pp** |
| **paired** two-sigma bar, full design era | **0.169pp** |
| **paired** two-sigma bar, champs slice | **0.364pp** |

**The review's 0.45pp was the level quantity** — the measured level bar is
0.445pp, reproducing it almost exactly, so it was the right number for the wrong
question. The correct paired bar is **2.6x tighter**, meaning the old bar was too
**high**: it would have rejected genuine improvements. This corrects the decision
rule for every future BPR contrast.

It also dissolves F-21. Anti-additivity's −0.503pp is **−5.95 sigma** against the
paired bar, not "within 0.02pp" of it.

P1's entire basis was one holdout slice: n=5,820, −1.84pp. On **twice** that
evidence, the deficit is −0.240pp with an interval spanning zero; on the
definition closest in size to the original slice, BPR is **ahead**; and BPR's
Brier beats EPA's at every champs definition. The holdout slice was noise.

Fitting a knob to it would have been fitting to holdout noise. **Task 4 was
skipped entirely** — no `spreadBeta` exists in `model.ts`, the port, or `tune.ts`
(`grep -rn spreadBeta packages/` → 0). Because Task 4 never ran, there was no
inert knob to retain or remove, unlike the Gate-C-failure branch.

**What was NOT disproved, and is worth a future look:** BPR's *lead* is absent at
champs — **+3.088pp off-champs versus ~0 at champs**. That contrast is real and
large. But "our lead shrinks here" is a different question from "we are behind
here", and it does not justify the mechanism P1 proposed. A supporting
measurement: anti-additivity's ablation delta *restricted to champs* is −0.104pp
[−0.471, 0.252], including zero — the mechanism P1 wanted to modulate is not
measurably load-bearing at champs to begin with.

## Corrections made during the work

Two test assertions I wrote were **over-claims** and were corrected by
measurement rather than by weakening them quietly:

- I first asserted match numbers ascend within an event. They do not: TBA's
  `sort_time` is genuinely non-monotonic at some events (2016ista played qm91–95
  before qm81–86; 107 such pairs in 2016 quals). Following actual play time is
  correct for a walk-forward replay.
- I then asserted the match-number *tiebreak*. The whole corpus holds exactly
  **one** adjacent same-event sort-time tie, so that assertion was vacuous. The
  test now pins the property that actually carries weight — the stream is
  globally non-decreasing in sort time — and records that **the ordering change
  was immaterial on this corpus; the population change is what moved the number.**
