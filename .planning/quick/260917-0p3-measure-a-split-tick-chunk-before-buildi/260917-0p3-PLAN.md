Measure whether a split-off teams-only chunk would fit in 10 ms, BEFORE building any tick splitting.

Purpose: Jacob's decision, 2026-09-17. The tick measures 17.5 ms mean / p50 16 on reused isolates
against a 10 ms budget whose enforcement turns on consistency. `pbTeams0` — the fold plus the event

cost, not an answer. If a chunk measures ~8 ms, splitting is worth building. If it measures 15 ms,
splitting moves the cost without fixing it.

These were read out of the source during planning. They are the load-bearing design answers; the

**F-1. The split boundary already exists in the tick.** `runPhaseBAndReport` writes the event
artifact first, then loops `realTouchedTeams` doing read guard, `mergeTeamSeasonArtifact`, write
(`apps/worker/src/scheduled.ts`, the loop around line 928). A split puts the boundary exactly
there: chunk one ends after the event write, chunk two is that loop.

**F-2. The consumer chunk reads the event artifact chunk one just wrote.** That artifact already
carries this tick's newly played rows, because chunk one merged them in. So the rows the consumer
needs are published data, not in-memory Phase A state — which is what makes a D1-free chunk
plausible at all.

**F-3. What `mergeTeamSeasonArtifact` actually reads off each `MatchResult`.** Through
`incrementRecord`: `eventType`, the two rosters, `winner`. Through `teamSeasonPlayedRow` and
`teamSeasonMatchRow`: `matchKey`, `eventKey`, `compLevel`, `setNumber`, `matchNumber`, the rosters,
`winner`, `redScore`, `blueScore`, `redRpEarned`, `blueRpEarned`. It reads **no** surrogate list and
**no** DQ list. The published `EventMatchSchema` played row carries every one of those as
`actualWinner`, `actualRedScore`, `actualBlueScore`, `actualRedRp`, `actualBlueRp` plus the schedule
fields, with `eventType` and `season` at artifact top level.

**F-4. The `Prediction` fields are published too, rounded.** `predictedWinner`, `pRedWin`,
`predictedRedScore`, `predictedBlueScore`, `redScoreVarianceOwn`, `blueScoreVarianceOwn`,
`redMatchBandVariance`, `blueMatchBandVariance`, `redRpPmf`, `blueRpPmf`, `matchOutcomePmf`,
`redBonusRpPmf`, `blueBonusRpPmf`, `redBonusRp`, `blueBonusRp`. Every one is the ROUNDED published
value, so a reconstructed prediction is re-rounded from an already-rounded number. That is a
fidelity note to report, not a blocker.

**F-5. The one genuine gap is the score breakdown.** `playedRowFactsFor` derives `actualBonusFlags`
through `actualBonusFlagsForMatch`, which needs either `hasScoreBreakdown` plus `scoreBreakdownRaw`
or a pre-parsed `ParsedBonusSides`. The published row carries neither — it carries the ANSWER,
`actualRedBonusRp` and `actualBlueBonusRp`, as boolean arrays ordered by the rule module's
`bonusNames`. So a consumer either inverts those arrays back into a `bonusFlags` record (possible;
needs the rule module) or accepts `null` flags and drops the published bonus columns. **This is the
design question the measurement exists to answer honestly, and the chunk must report which route it
took and what it cost.** It is NOT a reason to reach for D1.

**F-6. `sortTime` and `video` are published**, so `PlayedRowFacts`' other two fields come off the row.

**F-7. Team metrics and Sigma are published on the event artifact's `teams[]` rows**, written there
by `mergeEventArtifact` through `touchedEventTeamMetrics` — rounded, and a live tick's own rows carry
no `percentile`. So `metrics` and `sigmaAfterTick` are reconstructible without D1, with that loss.

**F-8. Net answer, to be confirmed by the instrument: a teams-only chunk needs NO D1 and no large
message payload.** A pointer message of event key, match keys and team keys is enough, which sits far
under the Queues 64 KB-per-operation billing unit. The chunk's subrequest count is one event GET plus
one GET per team — 13 for a twelve-team chunk — under 50 even on the strictest reading of the
subrequest cap.

**F-9. The subrequest-cap experiment does not belong in this probe, and is left out.** The probe's
safety rests on R2 and KV bindings being structurally absent from `wrangler.probe.toml`; adding an R2
binding to issue 50+ binding reads would destroy exactly that guarantee. And the probe's existing
outbound reads are public https fetches, which count against the documented 50-per-invocation
*fetch* limit — a different quantity from the undefined "internal services" 1,000, so 50+ of them
would answer a question nobody asked. If that ambiguity is worth settling, it wants its own
throwaway Worker with a read-only-by-usage R2 binding, deployed and deleted, never this one. F-8
notes that the ambiguity does not bind the design being measured here anyway.

**F-10. `chunk=event` is `pbTeams0`.** `pbTeams0` runs the full Phase A fold plus the event half of
Phase B with zero team merges — the cron-side chunk. Two differences, both immaterial to `cpuTime`:
it also issues one team-artifact GET the real cron chunk would not (fetch is I/O, billed as
subrequests, not CPU), and it does not pay the queue `send()` the real chunk would. So `chunk=event`
gets no second code path.

# ORCHESTRATOR-RUN (main context, network required)

Executor subagents' sandboxes deny all network Bash. Everything in this section runs from the main
context, after Task 1 and Task 2 are committed.

## The bar, pre-registered, stated BEFORE the run

**Splitting the tick is worth building only if the teams chunk lands comfortably under 10 ms on the
reused-isolate stratum: p50 at or under 8 ms AND mean at or under 9 ms.**

If it does not, splitting moves the cost without fixing it and the recommendation is to STOP — record
that as the finding and close the direction. A chunk in the 10 to 13 ms band is a failure of this
bar, not a near miss to be argued around after the fact; the whole point of writing the bar down
first is that it cannot be renegotiated once the number is in front of us. The one legitimate
follow-up on a miss is the specific one the zero-loop-count arm exists to expose: if the chunk's
fixed overhead dominates and the team merges themselves are cheap, the next question is a message
payload carrying the played rows rather than an event-artifact read — and that is a NEW measurement,
not a reinterpretation of this one.

## O1 — deploy and smoke, before any campaign

Deploy the Worker and the probe from the SAME commit, then the probe last:
`cd apps/worker && npx wrangler deploy --config wrangler.probe.toml`. Deploy from a clean tree; if
the tree is dirty, deploy from a clean detached worktree at the verified SHA. A probe built from a
different commit than the live Worker answers a different question.

Smoke each arm once with `curl`, using the rig's own common query so the roster and event overrides
are present, and read the response body before running anything longer. Confirm on the teams arm:
`discovery.queries` is 0; the algorithms list is empty; every fold counter is 0; merges run equals
twelve; matches reconstructed equals two; the counts of reconstructed predictions carrying the RP pmf
group and the band group are NON-ZERO. A zero on either of those last two means the published rows
are thinner than what `allPhaseB` merges and the comparison would be invalid — fix that before
spending an hour of wall clock, not after. Confirm on `chunk=event` that its warning fires and no
counter moved. Confirm an unrecognized value warns and skips nothing.

Check the day's D1 spend before and after: `npx wrangler d1 info sigmascout-state`, field
`rows_read_24h`. The chunk arms should add nothing to it.

## O2 — the cold pass

Run the driver from this quick task's `measure/` directory against the deployed probe: 30 s spacing,
at least 20 measured rounds per arm, warm-up excluded, arms interleaved round-robin. Run
`wrangler tail sigmascout-state-probe --format json` alongside it and keep the log — `cpuTime` comes
from the tail, never from a clock inside the probe, and the per-isolate request counter in the logs
is what separates fresh isolates from reused ones.

This is a long network run. Detach it properly — a backgrounded Bash run has been killed silently
mid-flight here before, at around six minutes, exiting 127 with a quiet log. Use a detached process
with a PID and a log file and poll it. A quiet log is not proof a run died; check the output file's
size and its modification time first.

Stop the tail by PID when the driver finishes. If auto-mode denies the tail, hand that step to Jacob.
