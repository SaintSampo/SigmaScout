# Quick task 260908-3k5: GBR — Gradient-Boosted Rating, a standalone research package

## What this is

A new FRC match-prediction model, structurally unrelated to anything already in this repo:
**hand-rolled histogram gradient-boosted trees over season-normalized walk-forward features**,
rolling-origin validated on 2016-2025 with a **sealed 2026 holdout**.

The scientific design was fixed by the orchestrator before this plan existed. **Do not redesign
it.** Everything the executor needs is in this document; no other design source should be
consulted. Where the design brief left a genuine gap, this plan closes it explicitly under a
`P-n` heading, with the reason — those are the only places a judgement call was made, and they
are the only places worth arguing with.

**Working name GBR** (id `"gbr"`, Gradient-Boosted Rating), sitting alongside OPR / EPA / VPR /
BPR.

## Why it is deliberately isolated

`packages/gbr/` copies the **discipline** of `packages/bpr/` (quick task `260908-b4t`), not its
model. Two contamination channels have to stay closed:

1. **State leakage** — closed by walk-forward, predict-strictly-before-update.
2. **Design leakage** — closed by tuning exclusively on 2016-2025 and never reading 2026.

  `HOLDOUT_YEARS = [2026]`.
- **2021 is not a gap to be filled.** There was no 2021 FRC season in this corpus's sense (remote

- **`holdout.ts` is not run by this task.** It is built, and it is proven to *refuse*. Running it

## Planner resolutions — gaps the brief left open

These are the only judgement calls in this plan. Each one closes a place where the brief is
silent or self-contradictory and an executor would otherwise guess.

### P-1 (BLOCKER) — use `tryParseBreakdownPair`, never bare `parseBreakdown`

The brief names `parseBreakdown`. **Using it would crash the run.** `parseBreakdown` propagates a
`ZodError` or `SyntaxError` for a malformed `score_breakdown`, and malformed breakdowns are a
large, measured population in exactly the data GBR streams — `breakdown/index.ts`'s own header
records **1,004 of 4,757** 2024 offseason matches carrying a breakdown that fails the parse. GBR
streams offseason matches (see P-5), so a bare `parseBreakdown` aborts partway through 2024.

Use `tryParseBreakdownPair(season, scoreBreakdownRaw)`, whose three-way outcome
(`absent` / `parsed` / `malformed`) is exactly the distinction the group-share features need. It
parses **both** alliances from one `JSON.parse`, and it still throws loudly for an unregistered
season — which cannot happen here, since all ten corpus seasons are registered in both
`SEASON_COMPONENT_MAPS` and `GROUPS_BY_SEASON` (verified 2026-09-08).

### P-2 — season score statistics get a warm-start prior

The brief says "running season score stats (Welford), floor sd at 1". At a season's first match
the accumulator has `count = 0`, so `mean = 0` and `sd = 1`, and the z-score of a 150-point
alliance is **+150**. Quantile binning makes that harmless as a *feature*, but the EWMAs are
state: one absurd z poisons a team's `marginZEwma` for a full halflife.

**Resolution:** at each season boundary, seed the new season's Welford accumulator with a
pseudo-count of **50** observations at the previous season's final `(mean, sd)` —
`count = 50, mean = prevMean, M2 = prevVar * 49`, which reproduces `prevSd` exactly. For the
first processed season (2016), seed `count = 0, mean = 0` with the sd floor of 1, and record in a
comment that 2016 is a training-only year in every origin so its ~50-match opening costs nothing.

This carries **scale**, not outcome information: for an eval on year Y the prior comes from
Y-1, which is entirely in the past. It does not weaken the season-normalization claim — within
minutes of week 1 the accumulator is dominated by the season's own data.

### P-3 — `sortTime` is dropped from the loaded row

The brief lists `sortTime` in the row shape, but `selectMatchesChronological` (which the brief
mandates) does not expose `sort_time` — it *consumes* it to produce a total chronological order
and returns `MatchResult`, which has no timestamp. Carrying a timestamp alongside an
already-ordered array would be two representations of one fact, which this project's D-21/D-10
convention forbids.

**The returned array order IS the chronological order.** `year` comes from the query argument,
not from the row.

| `district` | 1 | 74,278 |
| `champs` | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 | 26,453 |

### P-5 — the eligibility rule, stated once and used twice

The brief's paragraph on this contradicts itself midway and then resolves. The resolved rule:

> A match is **eligible** iff `winner` is `red` or `blue` **and** neither alliance carries a
> surrogate. **Every** match in the stream — ineligible ones included — updates state.
> **Only** eligible matches become training rows and only eligible matches are scored.
> Ties are counted and reported separately and are excluded from **both** accuracy and Brier.

Offseason matches are **in** the stream and **are** eligible — this repo's current accuracy
record is offseason-inclusive, so excluding them would make GBR's numbers incomparable to
everything else already published here.

**This diverges from `packages/bpr/evaluate.ts`, which folds ties into Brier at target 0.5.** The
divergence is deliberate. Do not "fix" it by copying BPR's `accumulate`.

One rule, two uses, so the training distribution and the evaluation distribution cannot drift
apart. The **eligible** column in the corpus table above is this exact count.

### P-6 — `noUncheckedIndexedAccess` is on, and it applies to typed arrays too

`tsconfig.json` sets `"noUncheckedIndexedAccess": true`. Verified 2026-09-08: this widens
**both** `number[]` and `Float64Array`/`Uint8Array` element reads to `number | undefined`. Every
hot loop in `gbdt.ts` will hit it.

**Policy:**
- In the GBDT hot loops (histogram accumulation, gradient/hessian sums, bin reads) use a non-null
  assertion at the read site. The repo already carries 607 of these under `packages/`, so this is
  house style, not a concession.
- **Never write `?? 0` on a gradient, hessian, histogram or bin array.** A silent zero there is
  indistinguishable from a real zero gradient and would corrupt the model with a green typecheck.
  `?? 0` is fine for genuinely-optional lookups (a missing component name in a parsed breakdown,
  a team not yet in the state map).
- Every index used with `!` must be provably in range from a loop bound in the same function.
  Say so in a one-line comment at each hot loop, not at each read.

### P-7 — `tune.ts` fits only the head the objective reads

The selection objective is mean winner accuracy across four origins, tie-broken on Brier.
Neither reads the margin or total head. Fitting all three heads during search would **triple**
the tuning cost for information the objective discards.

`tune.ts` therefore defaults to `--heads win`; `evaluate.ts` and `holdout.ts` default to
`--heads all`. It is a **flag**, not a hardcoded divergence, and the flag's value is written into
every results record so a run's scope is never ambiguous. This changes no model — the selected
parameters apply to all three heads unchanged.

### P-8 — "group z-share" means share, and needs no z

A group share is `groupPoints / (auto + teleop + endgame)`, a unitless fraction in `[0, 1]`. It
is already game-free and scale-free, which is the stated purpose of the z-scoring elsewhere;
z-scoring a fraction would add a second normalization for no gain. Ship the raw share.

Skip the group-EWMA update entirely (teams keep their previous value, initialized to 0) when
`tryParseBreakdownPair` returns `absent` or `malformed`, or when the denominator is `<= 0`, or
when any share is non-finite.

### P-9 — the feature vector is **25** features, enumerated

The brief estimates "~40 features"; the features it actually enumerates come to 25. Precision
beats matching the estimate. Ship 25, pin the count by equality in a test, and record in
`features.ts`'s header that 25 is the enumerated count and 40 was an estimate — so a future
reader does not go looking for 15 missing features.

**Per-team state, 14 values** (`t0..t13`), all maintained per team per season:

| idx | name | definition |
|---|---|---|
| t0 | `scoreZEwma` | EWMA of own alliance score, z-scored on the season's running stats |
| t1 | `marginZEwma` | EWMA of `(own - opp) / seasonSd` |
| t2 | `winEwma` | EWMA of the win indicator (1 win / 0 loss / 0.5 tie) |
| t3 | `scoreZMean` | running arithmetic mean of the same score z, this season |
| t4 | `marginZMean` | running arithmetic mean of the same margin z, this season |
| t5 | `winMean` | running arithmetic mean of the win indicator, this season |
| t6 | `autoShareEwma` | EWMA of the alliance's auto group share (P-8) |
| t7 | `teleopShareEwma` | EWMA of the teleop group share |
| t8 | `endgameShareEwma` | EWMA of the endgame group share |
| t9 | `oppStrengthEwma` | EWMA of the mean `marginZEwma` of the opposing teams faced |
| t10 | `prevSeasonRating` | carried across the season boundary (see below) |
| t11 | `veteranSeasons` | count of prior seasons in which this team played ≥ 1 match |
| t12 | `matchCount` | matches played this season, before this match |
| t13 | `matchesThisEvent` | matches played at the current event, before this match |

EWMA alpha is `1 - 0.5 ** (1 / halflife)`, halflife default **10** (tunable `{5, 10, 20}`).

**Per-alliance aggregate, 17 values:** `a0..a13` = mean of `t0..t13` over the alliance's teams;
`a14` = min of `t1`; `a15` = max of `t1`; `a16` = cold-start flag (1 iff every team on the
alliance has `t12 < 6`).

**Match feature vector, 25 values:**

| idx | feature | symmetric? |
|---|---|---|
| f0..f16 | `A_red[i] - A_blue[i]` for i = 0..16 | **NO — negates on swap** |
| f17 | `isElim` (`compLevel !== "qm"`) | yes |
| f18..f21 | event-class one-hot: regional, district, champs, offseason (P-4) | yes |
| f22 | mean `t13` over all six teams | yes |
| f23 | mean `t12` over all six teams | yes |
| f24 | `bothColdStart` (1 iff all six teams have `t12 < 6`) | yes |

`FEATURE_NAMES.length === 25`; the `SYMMETRIC` boolean mask has exactly **8** true entries. Both
pinned by **equality**, never by iteration — this repo has been bitten three times by tests that
iterate a hardcoded list and silently skip a new entry.

Alliances are not always three teams (2v2 offseason events exist). Aggregate over however many
team keys the row carries; if either alliance has zero teams, skip the match entirely and count
it in a reported `skippedNoTeams` counter.

**Labels**, computed with the season stats **as of extraction** (before this match is folded in),
so train-time and inference-time normalization are the same quantity:

- `win` = 1 if red won, 0 if blue (eligible rows only)
- `marginZ` = `(redScore - blueScore) / seasonSd`
- `totalZ` = `((redScore + blueScore) / 2 - seasonMean) / seasonSd`

Inverting at inference: `red = mean + (totalZ + marginZ/2) * sd`,
`blue = mean + (totalZ - marginZ/2) * sd`.

**Season boundary**, for every known team, in this order: `prevSeasonRating` becomes
`marginZEwma_final * prevSeasonDecay` if the team played ≥ 1 match in the season just ended,
otherwise `prevSeasonRating * prevSeasonDecay` (a stale value decays again rather than freezing);
`veteranSeasons` increments only if the team played; then every per-season field resets
(`matchCount`, `matchesThisEvent`, `currentEventKey`, all EWMAs, all running means). A rookie
enters with `prevSeasonRating = 0` and `veteranSeasons = 0`. `prevSeasonDecay` default **0.6**
(tunable `{0.3, 0.6, 0.9}`).

**Antisymmetry** is enforced two ways, and both are required. Structurally, the model sees only
difference features plus symmetric context, so an alliance swap negates the input. Explicitly, at
every inference site:

- win head: `p = (sigmoid(F(x)) + 1 - sigmoid(F(negate(x)))) / 2`
- margin head: `m = (G(x) - G(negate(x))) / 2`
- total head: `t = (H(x) + H(negate(x))) / 2`

where `negate` flips the sign of every non-symmetric feature and leaves the symmetric ones
untouched. The identity `p(swap) = 1 - p(x)` then holds algebraically, so the test is really
testing that `negate` matches the mask and that symmetrization is applied *everywhere*. Test both
halves: that a genuine alliance swap re-extracts to exactly `negate(x)`, and that the symmetrized
probability satisfies the identity within 1e-9.

**Forward-port note for a future `AlgorithmModule`:** never use event week or calendar dates.
`UpcomingMatch` does not carry them. `eventType` is available and is the only context field that
is.

3. **`features.ts`** — the season-transfer core. Implement exactly P-9's enumerated shape.
   - `SeasonState`: Welford `(count, mean, M2)` over **alliance scores** (each match contributes
     two observations), a `Map<string, TeamState>`, and the current season number.
     `seasonSd = max(sqrt(M2 / (count - 1)), 1)` with `count < 2` falling back to 1.

     throwing with the feature name on failure (`assertFiniteComponents`'s discipline).
   - `updateStates(state, match)` — the only function that reads `winner` / `redScore` /
     `blueScore` / `scoreBreakdownRaw`. Order inside it matters and must be commented: fold both
     alliance scores into the Welford accumulator **first**, then compute the z-quantities used
     for the EWMA updates from the **updated** stats, then update per-team state. (Feature

## Task 2 — Hand-rolled histogram gradient boosting

**Files:** `packages/gbr/gbdt.ts` (new), `packages/gbr/gbdt.test.ts` (new)

**Action:**

1. **Zero dependencies.** No `ml-matrix`, no new package. Typed arrays and arithmetic only. This
   file must stay pure enough to port into a Cloudflare Worker later — no Node built-ins in the
   prediction path.

2. **Binning.** `buildBinner(X: Float64Array, nRows, nFeatures, maxBins = 256)` returns per-feature
   ascending thresholds `t_0 < ... < t_{k-1}` with `k <= maxBins - 1`, computed as quantile cut
   points over the **training set only**. If a feature has `<= maxBins` distinct values, use
   midpoints between consecutive distinct values instead of quantiles. A constant feature gets zero
   thresholds, one bin, and can never be split.
   `binOf(f, x)` = the count of thresholds `t_j` with `x > t_j`, so `bin <= b` is **exactly**
   equivalent to `x <= t_b`. State that equivalence in a comment — it is the contract that makes
   train-time binned splits and inference-time raw splits the same model, and it is the single
   most likely place for a silent off-by-one.
   The binned design matrix is a row-major `Uint8Array` of length `nRows * nFeatures`
   (~5.6 MB at 141k × 25 — well inside budget).

3. **Boosting.** `fitForest(params, binned, binEdges, y, objective)`:
   - Objectives: `logistic` (`p = sigmoid(F)`, `g = p - y`, `h = p * (1 - p)`, base score
     `log(ȳ / (1 - ȳ))`) and `squared` (`g = F - y`, `h = 1`, base score `ȳ`).
   - Depth-wise growth to `maxDepth`. Split gain
     `0.5 * (GL²/(HL+λ) + GR²/(HR+λ) - G²/(H+λ))`, require `gain > 0`, require both children to
     meet `minChildWeight` on the hessian sum.
   - Leaf value `-G / (H + λ)`, **multiplied by `learningRate` and baked into the stored leaf**, so
     `predictForest` needs no parameters at all.
   - Histogram subtraction: build the histogram for the smaller child, derive the larger from
     `parent - sibling`. Halves the cost and is the difference between "a few minutes" and "too
     slow to tune".
   - `subsample` (rows, redrawn per tree) and `colsampleByTree` (features, drawn once per tree) via
     a deterministic **mulberry32** seeded `seed + treeIndex`, so a tree's draw does not depend on
     how many heads ran before it.
   - **Early stopping is OFF.** `nTrees` is fixed; rolling-origin selection handles capacity.
   - Params: `{ nTrees, maxDepth, learningRate, minChildWeight, lambda, subsample, colsampleByTree, seed }`.

4. **Serialization.** `serializeForest` produces plain JSON: `{ nFeatures, baseScore, trees: [...] }`
   where each tree is parallel arrays over node index — `feature` (`-1` for a leaf), `threshold`
   (the **raw** value `t_b`, not the bin index), `left`, `right`, `value`. `predictForest(model,
   x: readonly number[]): number` walks `x[feature] <= threshold ? left : right` and sums leaf
   values onto `baseScore`; it is pure, `O(depth)` per tree, and allocates nothing.

5. **Budget.** Must fit 141k rows × 25 features × 300 trees in a few minutes in Node. Print
   wall-clock per fit from `fitForest` behind a `verbose` flag — Task 3 needs that number to size a
   real tuning run.

**Verify:**

  1. **Learns interactions.** 4,000 rows, 4 features, `y = XOR(x0 > 0, x1 > 0)` with **5%** label
     noise and 2 pure-noise features. At `maxDepth 3, nTrees 100, learningRate 0.1`: train accuracy
     **> 0.90**. At `maxDepth 1` (stumps cannot represent XOR): train accuracy **< 0.60**. The

  2. **Determinism.** Same seed, two fits → `JSON.stringify(serializeForest(...))` **exactly**

  4. **Bin/raw agreement.** Predict every training row twice — once by walking the binned matrix
     with bin-index comparisons, once through `predictForest` on the raw `Float64Array` — and
     assert exact equality on all 4,000 rows. **This is the only detector for a threshold

  5. **Runtime.** The XOR benchmark completes in **< 30 s**; report the observed time.
