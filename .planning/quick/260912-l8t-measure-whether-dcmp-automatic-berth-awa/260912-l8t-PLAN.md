# Can we predict the DCMP awards that actually decide a Worlds berth?

At a District Championship, **Impact (0), Engineering Inspiration (9) and Rookie All Star (10) are not
points toward qualification — they ARE the qualification.** The award carries an automatic Worlds
berth. That makes them the one place in the award data where a prediction would directly serve
Jacob's actual end goal, Worlds/district lock prediction.

The deliverable is a measurement and a plain-language answer. **No model, no surface, no artifact, no
publish.**

## Why this is the right next question, and why it does not inherit the chain's conclusion

The opportunity was measured before this plan was written, and it is real:

- **519** automatic-berth awards (types 0, 9, 10) at DCMP events over **108 district-seasons**,
  2016-2026.
- **49.9% (259 of 519) went to a team OUTSIDE the district points cut.** For those teams the award is
  the entire reason they went to Worlds.
- That is **9.3% of all district Worlds berths** (259 of 2,798), trending to **11-14% in 2022-2026**.
- Concrete: 2016 CHS Impact to a team ranked **34th** with the cut at 25; 2016 FiM Rookie All Star to a
  team ranked **101st of 411** with the cut at 76.

**This case does not inherit the earlier "awards barely matter" conclusion.** Awards decide only 1.8%
of DCMP *qualifying* slots; this is ~5x larger and it is a different mechanism — not points, a berth.
The number nobody has measured is the one that matters: **are those berths predictable?**

## THE LOAD-BEARING REQUIREMENT — the stratification

The chain's central finding is that the model's entire predictive power comes from **prior
decoration**, which identifies perennial winners. Separately measured: award points land
overwhelmingly on teams that are already safely qualified — mean **16.39** award points for teams well
above the DCMP cut against **1.33** well below, a **12x gradient**.

So there is an obvious and dangerous failure mode:

> **The model may predict only the DCMP awards that go to already-qualified powerhouses, and be
> useless on exactly the ~50% that actually decide a berth. A pooled DCMP accuracy number would hide
> this completely.**

Therefore:

> **LOAD-BEARING REQUIREMENT.** Every DCMP result is reported **stratified by whether the actual
> winner was INSIDE or OUTSIDE the district points cut**, with the two strata printed separately and
> prominently. The **OUTSIDE** stratum is the one that answers Jacob's question. **A pooled-only
> number is not an acceptable deliverable.** This is enforced in code, not in prose: the DCMP block
> emits its pooled row and its stratum rows from one function, so a pooled headline cannot be printed
> without both strata beside it, and a test asserts that.

**Say the honest possible outcome out loud now, before any number is seen:** it is entirely plausible
that the answer is **"we can predict DCMP awards, but only the ones that don't matter."** If that is
what the numbers say, **that is the finding**, and the SUMMARY states it as flatly as it would state a
success. This plan is built so that outcome is reportable rather than embarrassing.

### The join bug that silently returns zero rows

This already cost one debug cycle. `events.district_key` holds the **BARE abbreviation** (`'chs'`).
`districts.district_key` is **YEAR-PREFIXED** (`'2024chs'`). `packages/corpus/schema.sql:195-206`
warns about exactly this. Joining the two columns directly matches **nothing** and fails **silently
with an empty result set, not an error** — producing a confident, wrong "no data" answer.

The correct join:

```sql
FROM events e
JOIN districts d ON d.abbreviation = e.district_key AND d.year = e.year
WHERE e.event_type IN (2, 5)
```

**Pin it with an assertion.** If the joined DCMP row count is below 100, **throw** with a message that
names the bare-vs-year-prefixed trap by name. Expected: **110 type-2 + 64 type-5 = 174** DCMP events
across the corpus. An empty join must be loud.

### The points cut is an APPROXIMATION and must be labelled as one

The cut used here is `district_rankings.rank <= districts.cmp_slots` for that district-year.

**That is not exact.** Automatic qualifiers (these very awards, plus Hall of Fame and other
pre-qualified teams) consume Worlds slots, so the true points cut sits **slightly higher** — fewer
points-based berths are actually available than `cmp_slots` suggests. The direction of the error is
known: **the 49.9% / 9.3% figures above are CONSERVATIVE and understate the OUTSIDE share.**

Say that in the output header, not only in the SUMMARY. Do not imply exactness anywhere.
`packages/core/districts/prequalified.ts` exists and would refine this — **do not import it, do not
touch it** (see Out of scope); name the refinement in "Owed, not done here".

## Tasks

### T1 — the district cut, and the stratum every DCMP award belongs to

`scripts/measureAwardPredictability.ts` + its test file. One commit.

**The loader.** A new `loadDistrictCuts(db)` reading, in one query, the correct join above:

```
dcmpEventKey -> { districtKey, year, cmpSlots: number | null, rankByTeam: Map<teamKey, rank> }
```

Assertions, all of which throw rather than returning an empty result:

1. The joined DCMP event count is **≥ 100** (expected 174). The throw message names the
   bare-abbreviation / year-prefixed trap explicitly, because that is the failure this assertion
   exists to catch.
2. At least one district-year returns a non-empty `rankByTeam`.

Counted-and-printed, **never thrown**: district-seasons with a null `cmp_slots`, and district-seasons
with **zero** `district_rankings` rows. If either count is large it caps what this measurement can
say, and the reader must see it.

**The stratum.** `type Stratum = "INSIDE" | "OUTSIDE" | "UNKNOWN"`, assigned per instance from the
instance's **recipients**, in this order — the order matters and is the whole rule:

1. **UNKNOWN** if the event has no district row, `cmp_slots` is null, or **any** recipient has no
   `district_rankings` rank. "Cannot tell" is never silently folded into either answer.
2. **OUTSIDE** if **any** recipient has `rank > cmp_slots` — the award decided a berth for someone.
3. **INSIDE** otherwise.

The stratum is a fact about **the award**, not about the candidate pool, so it is assigned from
`instance.recipients` regardless of pool membership. An unreachable instance therefore still lands in
a stratum and still scores 0 at every k, consistent with the two-denominator rule i13 already
established.

**The premise check, which is a control and not a formality.** Print the stratifier's own count of
type-{0, 9, 10} DCMP instances and the OUTSIDE share, against the pre-measured **519 / 49.9%**.
Instances merge same-type awards at one event, so the instance count may sit slightly below 519.
**If the count is outside ±10% of 519, or the OUTSIDE share is outside 45-55%, STOP** — the join or
the cut is wrong and nothing downstream is trustworthy. Diagnose; do not "explain" it.

**Note explicitly, in the output:** the 2016 examples that motivated this task (CHS Impact rank 34 /
cut 25; FiM Rookie All Star rank 101 of 411 / cut 76) are in the **first season, which is
training-only and never scored**. They appear in the census and not in the scored tables. A reader
must not go looking for them in the results.

**Tests** (synthetic fixtures, no corpus):

- **The join bug, asserted both ways.** A fixture where `events.district_key = 'chs'` and
  `districts.district_key = '2024chs'`: the naive column-to-column join returns **zero** rows and the
  `abbreviation + year` join returns rows. This is the test that proves the trap is closed rather
  than avoided by luck.
- The count assertion throws on an empty/short join, with the trap named in the message.
- Stratum precedence: UNKNOWN beats OUTSIDE beats INSIDE, on fixtures where each pair collides —
  including a multi-recipient instance with one inside and one unknown recipient (→ UNKNOWN) and one
  inside and one outside (→ OUTSIDE).
- Null `cmp_slots` → UNKNOWN, counted, never treated as zero capacity and never treated as unlimited.
- A recipient missing from `district_rankings` → UNKNOWN, not INSIDE.
- `rank === cmp_slots` is INSIDE and `rank === cmp_slots + 1` is OUTSIDE — the boundary is asserted,
  not assumed.
- Type 5 division events resolve to the same district-year as their type 2 sibling.

### T2 — score narrow, train wide, and report both strata

Same two files, second commit. Depends on T1; **must not run in parallel with it or in a separate
worktree** — same files, same checkout.

**TRAIN WIDE, SCORE NARROW, and the reason is not convenience.** DCMP instances are thin — roughly
170 per award type across 9 scored seasons. Restricting **training** to DCMP only would drop below
`THIN_PRIOR_INSTANCES = 30` for the early seasons, collapse both arms into the B1 fallback, and
**measure the fallback instead of the model**. So:

- `input.instances`, `historyBefore` / `buildPriorHistory`, `trainPool`, both `fitConditionalLogit`
  calls and `isThinPrior` are **completely untouched**. Prior decoration still counts awards from
  every event type at every event.
- The narrowing happens **only** at accumulation.

**This is the single most dangerous line in the task.** Filtering `input.instances` instead would
quietly redefine prior decoration as "prior DCMP decoration" — a different model wearing this one's
name. Guard it with a test: **the fitted weights under `--dcmp` are bit-identical to the weights
under the default run**, asserted on a fixture, per `(awardType, season)`.

**The mechanism: extend the fan-out list, do not add a second pass.** `runExperiment`'s
`for (const target of [cell, report.pooled])` becomes a list that additionally contains the stratum
cell and the DCMP-pooled cell **when a stratum is assigned and `--dcmp` is on**. Consequences, all of
which are free and all of which are the point:

- One pass, one fit, shared by every stratum **by construction** rather than by assertion.
- Every existing accumulator — hits, `RankStats`, `CalibrationStats`, `poolSum`, `unreachable`,
  `thinPriorRows`, the exact B0 — works on a stratum cell with zero new accumulation code.
- **`DCMP-ALL` = `INSIDE` + `OUTSIDE` + `UNKNOWN` exactly**, on `n` and on every hit count. Assert it.

*(The rejected alternative — three filtered `runExperiment` runs — would have cost 3x and turned the
shared-fit guarantee from structural into a test. Named so it is not re-proposed.)*

`AwardTypeReport` gains `byStratum?: Map<Stratum | "DCMP-ALL", Cell>`; `ExperimentReport` gains an
optional `dcmp` section carrying the stratum census and the cut caveat. Both **optional**, both absent
without the flag.

**Constraint 8 is a hard gate: the default output must stay byte-identical.** Before making any edit,
capture `pnpm measure:award-predictability > <baseline>` to a path **outside the working tree** (the
session scratchpad or the OS temp dir — never inside the repo, so it cannot be staged). After T2,
re-run and **`diff` must be empty**. If it is not, the DCMP work has leaked into the default path;
stop and fix it rather than accepting the diff. The **R@1-equals-accuracy identity on all 156 pooled
predictor rows** remains the control for the whole script and must still hold.

**Output — a new DCMP block, printed only under `--dcmp`, after the existing output.**

Per award type, headlining **0 (Impact), 9 (Engineering Inspiration), 10 (Rookie All Star)** as
`BERTH_AWARD_TYPES` — the three that carry an automatic Worlds berth. Other judged types present at
DCMPs print below them for context. Types 1 and 2 stay in the existing `REFERENCE_ONLY_AWARD_TYPES`
(`:147`) section and headline nothing.

For each type, rows in this order — **OUTSIDE first, because it is the answer**:

```
OUTSIDE   n=NN   pool=PP   [rank metrics + B0 + reference predictor]
INSIDE    n=NN   pool=PP   [same]
UNKNOWN   n=NN   pool=PP   [same]
DCMP-ALL  n=NN   pool=PP   [same]   <- never printed alone
```

Requirements on that block, each of which exists to stop a specific misreading:

1. **The pooled row cannot be emitted without the strata.** One function emits all four rows. A test
   asserts that any output containing `DCMP-ALL` also contains `OUTSIDE` and `INSIDE`.
2. **Print mean pool size per stratum, and B0 per stratum.** DCMP pools are much larger than district
   pools (FiM ~160+ vs ~40) and are already filtered to a district's best, so the random baseline is
   lower **and** prior decoration is less discriminating because everyone in the room is good.
   **A changed baseline is not a changed model**, and the output must make that impossible to
   confuse: every recall number is printed next to its own stratum's B0.
3. **Print raw counts beside every percentage** — `41.2% (31 of 75)`. At n≈75 a bare percentage
   invites over-reading.
4. **Print the one-instance granularity per stratum**, and use it. The measured bands in
   `RANK_NOISE_BANDS` capture **optimizer** variance over the full instance set; at n≈75 a single
   instance is **1.3pp**, which is comparable to or larger than the R@3 band. A new
   `stratumBand(metric, n)` returns `max(measuredBand, 2 instances)` in the band's own unit, and the
   DCMP block scores against that instead of the raw band. `null` stays `null` — an unmeasured metric
   still prints **CANNOT BE SCORED** and never borrows a granularity floor as a band.
   `compareOnBand`'s existing behaviour and all existing output are untouched.
5. **`THIN-n` on any stratum with n < 30**, with no conclusion permitted from it — stated in the block
   header, not only in the SUMMARY.
6. **Type 10 is read against RB1/RB2** via the existing `rankReferencePredictor`; types 0 and 9
   against B1/B2. Enforced in code, as i13 already does.
7. **The cut caveat prints in the block header**: the cut is `rank <= cmp_slots`, automatic qualifiers
   consume slots, so the true cut is higher and the OUTSIDE share reported here is conservative.

**Tests:**

- `DCMP-ALL` equals the sum of the three strata on `n` and on every predictor's hit count.
- Weight identity between the default run and `--dcmp`, per `(awardType, season)`.
- A non-DCMP instance contributes to **no** stratum cell, and a DCMP instance contributes to exactly
  one stratum cell plus `DCMP-ALL`.
- `stratumBand` floors to the two-instance value when n is small and returns the measured band when n
  is large; `null` bands stay `null`.
- The pooled-requires-strata invariant.
- Recall@1 still equals top-1 accuracy **within each stratum cell** — the i13 control, re-asserted at
  stratum granularity.
- The leak tests extended over the stratum state: season Y's stratified numbers are identical whether
  or not seasons ≥ Y exist beyond it. (Ranks and `cmp_slots` are end-of-season district facts, so this
  is a check that the stratum never reaches the *feature* path — it is an assertion, not a formality.)

**Pre-commit the readings now, before any number is seen:**

1. **The OUTSIDE stratum is the answer. The INSIDE stratum is context.** Any headline sentence that
   quotes a pooled DCMP number without both strata is a failed execution whatever the numbers say.
2. **Predictable on a berth award** only if the reference predictor's (or the model's) **R@3 on the
   OUTSIDE stratum** beats that stratum's **B0** by more than `stratumBand("R@3", n)`. B0 is the
   exact random null for that stratum's own pool size — a lower baseline at a bigger pool is not a
   win.
3. **If OUTSIDE is at or near random while INSIDE is strong, the finding is: "we can predict DCMP
   awards, but only the ones that don't matter."** State it in exactly that register. That is a real
   result about Jacob's end goal and it closes the question rather than failing it.
4. **Berth-level framing, not just top-1.** Recall@k on OUTSIDE is the shippable capability even when
   top-1 is poor: if a top-5 list at a DCMP contains the eventual award-decided berth-winner a useful
   fraction of the time, that is worth saying — with the raw count, and with the ~PP-team pool named
   so `R@5` on a 160-team FiM field is not read as `R@5` on a 40-team field.
5. **Type 10 against RB1/RB2, never B1/B2.** A rank "win" over a structurally near-bottom baseline is
   7bp's artifact in a fourth outfit.
6. **A gap inside `stratumBand` prints as "no difference", never "slightly better."** Same discipline
   the chain has applied since 5n8, now with the small-n floor that the measured bands do not cover.
7. **The cut is approximate and conservative.** Every quoted OUTSIDE share carries that qualifier.

**The SUMMARY's first paragraph answers Jacob's question in plain words:** can we predict the DCMP
awards that decide a Worlds berth — with the measured OUTSIDE R@1 and R@3, the raw counts, the pool
size, the reference predictor beside them, and the INSIDE number for contrast. **A negative answer is
a result and gets stated as flatly as a positive one.**
