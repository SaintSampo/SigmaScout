## Lever decisions (planner's, on the numbers above)

1. Generator hot-loop rewrite: PLANNED FIRST. Largest measured payoff, helps cold runs, CI and every machine, and leaves no persistent state behind that could go stale.
2. The 128-cell cap: MEASURED, and raised only if the census exceeds it (rule A4).
3. On-disk structure cache: NOT BUILT here. It is the one lever that adds a stale-state path to published bytes, and after lever 1 its payoff is about a minute per 2026 run. Rule A3 re-checks that premise on measured numbers and reports it; this task builds no cache whatever the number says.
4. Worker threads: NOT BUILT, same rule.

## Pre-registration, Part A (fixed before any run)

- A1, identity bar: zero differing bytes. If the rewrite cannot be made identical, the generator file is restored and the lever is dropped. A golden digest is never edited to make a rewrite pass.
- A2, timing is read within a run. Generation seconds come from the sweep, old and new interleaved in one process. For the full runs, the old-generator arm and the new-generator arm are launched together so they see the same machine; each reports EPA sidecars (cold memo) and SPR sidecars (warm memo); SPR is the control. Absolute seconds are one machine's numbers.
- A3, on-disk cache rule: compute the sweep's new-generator seconds over the 2026 cell set divided by the new arm's EPA plus SPR sidecar seconds. At 10% or more, FINDINGS says a cache is still worth a follow-up task. Under 10%, it says the cache is not worth its stale-state risk. Planner's expectation: about 5%.
- A4, cap rule: if one all-seasons run reaches more than 128 distinct cells, raise `SCHEDULE_STRUCTURE_CACHE_CELLS` to 512 (or the next power of two above the census count if that count is over 400). At 128 or fewer, leave the constant alone and record the count in FINDINGS only.

## Pre-registration, Part B: frame F (fixed before any Part B number exists)

Method: the binding resampling floor. The same construction is built twice (sides A and B) with independent shuffle and draw streams, by suffixing `algorithmVersion`, which feeds the `shuffle` and `baked` seed strings and nothing else. Pricing is one bound `predict` on both sides. Pairing structures are the shared deterministic ones on both sides, as in production.

- Sample: 2026txmca, 2026casnd, 2026joh (27, 40 and 75 teams at 260929-mkn's extraction; 142 teams pooled). Algorithm: spr at its current version, pre-event walk-forward state rebuilt by `buildDistrictPricingState` cut at each event's first played match over 2016-2020 and 2022-2025, Sigma carry and RP cold prior on.
- Replicates: R = 10 independent pairs per cell, suffixes `#v3h-r0A` ... `#v3h-r9B`, the same suffixes at every count, so a smaller count's schedules are a prefix of a larger count's.
- Statistics come from the shipped code: `PublishedPreScheduleArtifactSchema.parse`, `decodePreScheduleResult`, `buildRankDistributionRows`, `rankBandLabel`. Never a second quantile estimator.
- Main arm: n in 100, 150, 200, 300, 500, 750, 1000, 2000 at 50 draws per schedule.
- Secondary arm, total draws fixed at 50,000: 250 x 200 and 500 x 100, with 250 x 50 as its own control and the main arm's 500 x 50 and 1000 x 50 as comparators.

What the page can show, read from the client code:
- The median column prints an integer: the continuous median rounded half up and clamped to 1..teamCount. A continuous difference of 1.0 or less moves the printed integer by at most one step; 2.0 or less, by at most two. No noise level guarantees an unchanged integer, because a median near x.5 flips under any noise. So "invisible" is measured, as the share of printed medians that do not change.
- The band label prints the 10th and 90th percentiles to one decimal. Only differences under 0.05 rank are invisible there.
- Rows sort by the continuous median, so near-tied neighbours can swap under any noise.
- The scope line prints the schedule count and total draws from the sidecar itself.

Gates. On the main arm, a count n is ACCEPTABLE when all three hold:
- G1: pooled share of teams with |delta median| at most 1.0, mean over the 10 pairs, is at least 95%. One printed step for at least 19 teams in 20.
- G2: the largest |delta median| over every event, team and pair is at most 2.0. No printed median ever moves three steps.
- G3: pooled share of 10th-percentile edges within 1.0 rank is at least 90%, and the same for 90th-percentile edges (rung 1's clause 2, unchanged).

The thresholds come from the display's own steps (1.0 and 2.0 ranks) and the rung-1 criterion's own shares (95% and 90%). A gate statistic within 1 percentage point (G1, G3) or 0.1 rank (G2) of its bar is reported as "at the bar" and counts as not passed.

Reading:
- The recommendation names the smallest ACCEPTABLE n on the grid. n = 1000 is the reference row. If 1000 itself misses a gate, FINDINGS says so in plain words and reports the smallest passing count as information only.
- "More draws buy back fewer schedules" is claimed only if 250 x 200 or 500 x 100 is ACCEPTABLE where the same n at 50 draws is not.
- Descriptive only, never gating: share within 0.5; mean |delta median|; worst team as the mean over pairs; share of identical printed medians; share of printed medians two or more apart; share of identical band labels; share of unchanged row positions; largest row displacement; |delta median| divided by the team's own band width (median and 95th percentile).
- Seconds: per event, generation seconds at first touch and price-plus-draw seconds per build at each n. Any season figure is the measured 2026 SPR sidecar seconds scaled linearly in n and labelled extrapolated.
- The decision to change the count is Jacob's. It would ship as a new spr version AND a new epa version, because both algorithms' sidecar bytes change. EPA's floor is not measured here.
