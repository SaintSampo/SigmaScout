# Raise `PRESIM_SCHEDULE_COUNT` from 20 to 1,000

Step 2 of the pre-schedule count work. **The number is decided; this plan does not relitigate it.**

## Why

At the shipped 20, two runs of the *identical* construction disagree on 73% of teams and move the
worst team **10.61 ranks** — the published rank band is mostly sampling noise. At 1,000 the worst
team moves **1.17** ranks and the pooled mean is **0.275**, below what a reader can perceive on an
integer scale. 4,000 was considered and declined: 1.17 → 0.71 is invisible on that scale and costs
another hour on every republish.

## Why it is safe only now

`260912-2ur` dropped the priced `schedules` block from the published sidecar. With the block baked,
a sidecar at 1,000 would have been ~18 MB — roughly **11.5 GB** of presim across the 641 sidecars a
publish writes, against a **10 GB** R2 free tier. Aggregate-only it is ~24 KB, so the artifact still
ships **smaller than it does today at 20**.

## Tasks

### T1 — the constant and its rationale comment

`packages/harness/publish.ts:153` — `PRESIM_SCHEDULE_COUNT` 20 → 1000.

`:155`'s comment on `PRESIM_DRAWS_PER_SCHEDULE` currently justifies the draw count by matching the
client's `SIMULATION_DRAWS`. **That rationale stops being true** at 1,000 × 50 = 50,000 baked draws.
Rewrite rather than renumber. The replacement must say:

- `drawsPerSchedule` stays at **50** because pricing dominates drawing ~4:1 — measured 2026-09-12,
  drawing is 23.5% of per-schedule cost at 75 teams and 20.9% at 18 — so cutting draws to fund more
  schedules buys ~1.11× on the binding floor *and* costs draw-side resolution.
- the baked draw count **no longer matches** the client's `SIMULATION_DRAWS`, and that is intended:
  the baked path estimates an over-all-schedules distribution and wants more samples; the live path
  simulates one known schedule.
