<objective>
Add the search bar that reaches every team and every event from anywhere, then close the phase: remove
the throwaway spike, measure what was deferred, and put the whole thing in front of a human on a real
phone.

Purpose: NAV-03 is the dominant use case — "find my team" — and D-08 and D-09 already fixed both the
interaction and the matching rule, so the interesting parts are the two the research flagged: the
matching predicate is the one place in this phase where user-typed text is evaluated on every keystroke,
and D-10's lazy fetch means the dropdown has two degraded states that must not take the resident team
results down with them.

This plan is also the phase's closing pass. Plan 05-04 deferred the keystroke-latency gate until a
search box existed; the touch spike was always throwaway; NAV-05's deep-link promise has been asserted
in unit tests but never end to end in a real browser; and the UI contract lists three manual-only
verifications that need a person and a phone.

Output: a tested matching module, the search dropdown with its two degraded states, a scripted deep-link
proof, the deferred measurement, a spike-free bundle, and a human sign-off.
</objective>

Compose the dropdown on top of the tested matcher, and make the two degraded states real rather than
theoretical.

**Lazy fetch (D-10).** On first focus or first keystroke, enable the query for whichever artifact the
current route does not already hold — the events artifact on the Teams page, the teams artifact on the
Events page. Not at mount, and not at page load: D-10 chose lazy precisely so the first paint is not
charged for a feature the visitor may never use. Pass the resulting status into `buildSearchResults` so
the model, not the component, decides what the events group is.

**Rendering.** Matches group under small section labels for teams and events, capped at eight total, no
internal scroll and no see-more affordance. Keyboard navigation moves through the combined list;
Enter navigates to the highlighted row, or to the top hit when nothing is highlighted, which is what
makes the exactly-one-match case a single keystroke with no disambiguation step. The highlighted row
uses the accent at a tint, per the contract's reserved-for list. An event row whose week is null renders
without a week chip — not a blank slot and not a literal null. Team nicknames and event names truncate
with an ellipsis mid-row and expose the full text through the native title affordance.

**The three event-section copies.** Loaded renders results, or the no-matches copy when the query
matches nothing anywhere. Loading renders the contract's loading copy in the events section only —
team results are resident and must render immediately, unblocked. Failed renders the contract's
team-results-only copy, still in the events section only. In neither degraded case does the whole search
fail, and in neither case does the arrival or failure of the events section reorder the team results or
move focus.

**Navigation target.** Team and event detail pages are Phases 6 and 7. Until they exist, a team result
navigates to the Teams page with that team's row scrolled into view and highlighted, and an event result
navigates to the Events page filtered to that event's week — both preserving year and algorithm through
the updater form. State this in a comment as the interim target with a pointer to the phase that
replaces it, so Phase 6 changes one destination rather than rediscovering the decision.

**Wire it.** Fill the ribbon's reserved search slot with the box on desktop and behind the existing icon
trigger on phone.

**Tests.** With the events query pending: team results render and are keyboard-navigable, the events
section shows the loading copy, and resolving the events query afterwards neither reorders the team rows
nor moves focus off the highlighted one. With the events query failed: team results still render and
navigate by keyboard, the events section shows the team-results-only copy, and no error banner appears
anywhere. Plus: a query matching nothing renders the no-matches copy inline; a query matching exactly
one row navigates on Enter with no intermediate step; nine matches render eight rows; an event row with
a null week renders no week chip; the mobile icon trigger exposes an accessible name.
