## D-09's bar, pinned here and committed as code BEFORE any reporting-slice number exists

This section is the plan's spine. Task 1 turns it into `evaluateD09Bar` and `decideRpShipConfig` and
commits them; Task 2 runs them and is forbidden from changing them. **Nothing below may be adjusted
after a 2023-2026 figure has been seen** — adjusting the bar to fit the result is the one failure
this plan's whole structure exists to prevent, and it would leave the suite green.

**Unit of account.** One *scored bonus cell* is one `(algorithmId, season, bonusName)` triple on the
reporting slice, with at least one observation in **both** arms. The reporting seasons carry 10
bonuses between them (2023 two, 2024 two, 2025 three, 2026 three) and `PUBLISHED_ALGORITHM_IDS` has
three members, so a fully-scored table is **30 cells**. Derive both numbers from
`RP_RULE_MODULES[season].bonusNames` and `PUBLISHED_ALGORITHM_IDS` at run time; do not hardcode 10 or
30 anywhere, because a test that iterates a hardcoded list silently skips a newly-registered season
while only an equality pin fails loudly.

**Per-cell outcome.** A cell *improves* when `brier_arm < brier_control` strictly. It *regresses*
when `brier_arm > brier_control` strictly. It *ties* when the two compare equal under `===`.

**The bar.** An arm meets it when `improved > scored / 2` **and** `regressed === 0`. Ties stay in
`scored` and count toward neither side, so a tie makes the majority harder to reach — the
conservative direction, chosen deliberately.

**Why no minimum effect size is honest here.** D-11 says any improvement in the right direction
counts and declined the paired-bootstrap interval. That is defensible in this specific measurement
because **09-04 deleted the Monte Carlo**: both arms are exact deterministic computations over the
same observations, differing only in the config field under test. There is no sampling scatter for an
effect-size floor to protect against, and the ±0.008 of noise that would have justified one is
precisely what the phase removed. State this reasoning in `docs/models/rp-attribution.md`; a reader
is entitled to know why an unusually permissive bar is not sloppy.

**Applying it per field (D-10).** The rule runs in three pre-committed steps, and `decideRpShipConfig`
implements exactly these and nothing else:

1. **Per-field gate.** Each single-change arm (`win`, `tie`, `marginal`) is evaluated against
   `control`. Every field whose arm meets the bar enters the provisional accept set.
2. **Combination gate.** The arm formed from the provisional accept set is itself evaluated against
   `control` and must also meet the bar — three changes that each help alone can interact badly, and
   the combination is what would actually ship. If it fails, drop the single field with the **smallest
   number of improved cells** (ties broken by the field's own arm name in the fixed order `marginal`,
   `tie`, `win`, so the rule is deterministic) and evaluate the reduced combination once. **At most
   one drop is permitted.** If the reduced combination still fails, the decision is `control` for all
   three fields.
3. **Leave-one-out, reported and not gating.** Each two-change arm's verdict is recorded so a reader
   can see each field's marginal contribution, but no step of the rule reads it.

That is at most three evaluations of the reporting slice, all defined before any of them ran, which
is what bounds D-04's one-way spend to a single event even though the table has eight arms in it.

**What the checkpoint may still do.** The developer may override the mechanical verdict per field.
That is a deliberate, recorded override, not a re-run of the rule, and the override and its reason go
into `docs/models/rp-attribution.md` beside the mechanical result.

## The two control arms — and which one D-09's bar uses

**This is a flagged planner finding; read it before writing any comparison.**

There are two candidate "before" instruments in this phase and they are not interchangeable:

- **09-01's `data/baselines/rp-calibration-2026-09.json`** was frozen in wave 1, before 09-04 replaced
  the 4,000-draw Monte Carlo with the closed form. 09-04's own plan states that every published pmf

  modelling arm on the shipped engine.
- **The `control` arm measured at HEAD in this plan** is the all-legacy `RpLayerConfig` running on
  09-04's closed form.

**D-09's per-bonus bar uses the `control` arm, not the file.** D-10 ships the closed form
unconditionally, so the engine swap is not on trial; the only question the bar governs is whether
each modelling change earns its place *given* the closed form. Scoring an arm against the frozen file
would credit or blame the modelling changes for the Monte Carlo's removal, and would additionally
compare a HEAD-computed number against one produced by code that no longer exists — the exact
cross-generation mismatch D-11's mitigation exists to prevent.

**09-01's file keeps two real jobs**, both of which this plan does use:

1. It is the left-hand side of the **phase-level F2 narrative** — "published bonus probabilities
   under-predicted 2.06x; here is where they are now" — and every such comparison must be **labelled
   cross-generation** wherever it appears, in the SUMMARY and in `docs/models/rp-attribution.md`.
2. It is the **engine-swap sanity cross-check**: the difference between it and the `control` arm is
   attributable to the closed form alone, and reporting that difference per bonus is free evidence
   about 09-04's work. It is reported, never gated on.
