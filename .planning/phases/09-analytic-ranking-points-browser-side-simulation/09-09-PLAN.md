<objective>
**Stop generating schedules. The 20 of them were a Monte Carlo over schedule randomness, and the thing they were approximating has a closed form too.**

Today a pre-schedule sidecar is built by drawing 20 synthetic qualification schedules from a licensed cheesy-arena template grid, pricing every synthetic match in every one of them through the real RP path, publishing all 20 priced schedules, and baking a rank distribution over them. The result is roughly 265 KB per event, of which 95.4% is priced schedules the client never reads (`docs/simulation-architecture.md`), and it is unreachable anyway: every presim object in R2 belongs to retired `vpr` (F11, D-20).

D-16 names what those 20 schedules actually are: **a Monte Carlo approximation of an expectation over schedule randomness — the same insight as the RP pmf, one level up.** If schedule randomness is averaged away in the end, concrete schedules were never the thing that was needed. What is needed is the distribution a random schedule induces, and that can be built directly: summarise the event's roster into field-level statistics — the mean *and the variance* of per-team contributions across that roster, which is what carries both match-to-match noise and partner-quality spread — build each team's field-averaged per-match pmf from its own belief plus those statistics, and convolve `matchesPerTeam` copies of it for the season total. Target: `{roster[], matchesPerTeam, perTeamPmf[]}` at roughly **2-3 KB**, with schedule generation and the template dependency **deleted entirely**, and the licence question evaporating because nothing template-derived is published any more (D-19).

**This is a laddered spike, not a fixed build (D-17).** Rung 1 is built, measured against the baked 20-schedule output on real events, and shipped only if it holds. Rung 2 — self-generated random schedules at ~20 KB, which would answer whether cheesy-arena's balanced-grid structure actually matters to final rank bands or is merely tradition — is **not planned here** and is built only if rung 1 fails. D-18 kept this in Phase 9 rather than splitting it out, so the whole ladder step lands in one plan.

Three things make this narrower than it looks, and one makes it wider.

**Narrower.** The Monte Carlo stays for the rank step (D-15, developer-confirmed, 09-07's business). `simulateRanks` is not modified here at all — the rung-1 arm hands it a different set of rows and nothing else. `analyticRpPmf` is not modified either; the field-averaged path calls the same function every real match calls, which is what keeps the C-04 structural guarantee ("no pricing math lives in the sidecar builder") true after the rewrite rather than merely re-promised.

**Wider.** The comparison has to be *fair*, and fairness here is a measurement problem, not a coding one. Both arms must be produced from one replay, one model state, one layer, the same `simulateRanks` and the same `continuousQuantile` — the `measureRewindGap.ts` convention — and the pass/fail bar must be fixed **before** the numbers are seen. That is why the acceptance criterion below is quoted verbatim rather than re-derived, why it is encoded as code in the measurement script, and why the `checkpoint:decision` sits *after* the measurement presenting its verdict rather than standing in for it.

**And it is one-way.** Deleting schedule generation and the template dependency breaks the published sidecar shape; restoring it means a schema change plus a republish (D-16). So the ship/no-ship call is a blocking `checkpoint:decision` rated `one-way`, and everything downstream of it — the deletion, the client swap, the content contract handed to 09-10 — lives in a single task behind that gate.

Purpose (D-16/D-17/D-18/D-19, deliverable 8's measurement, F11's content half): decide, on measured evidence, whether concrete schedules are needed at all.

Output: a field-averaged predictor, a measured verdict against the baked output on at least six real finished events, a recorded one-way decision, and — on SHIP — a 2-3 KB sidecar shape handed to 09-10 with schedule generation gone.
</objective>

## The rung-1 construction, specified

**PATTERNS.md found NO analog for this math.** Its own "No Analog Found" row says so and names the building blocks: `RpMomentsAccumulator`, `analyticPmf`'s convolution machinery, `simulateRanks`'s zero-baseline mode. What follows is therefore new design composed from those, written out here so an executor transcribes rather than invents. **It is the checklist, not a sketch.**

Let `A = ALLIANCE_SIZE = 3`. Let the event's roster be the `n` teams, and index threshold variables by `v` in `ruleModule.thresholdVariables` order.

### Step 1 — one contribution per team

For team `t`, `buildFieldContributions` reads four numbers, each from the instant the existing baked path already reads it from:

| Field | Source | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `variableMeans[v]` | `rpAccumulator.momentsFor([t], 0, 0).meanVector[v]` | a ONE-TEAM roster |
| `variableVariances[v]` | the same call's `varianceBlock[v][v]` | same call, read the diagonal |
| `scoreMean` | `teamTotals.get(t)` — `teamMetrics(pricingState, roster)[t][TOTAL_METRIC_KEY].value` | the algorithm's own per-team total |
| `bandVariance` | `consistencyByTeam().get(t)` squared | Sigma Score for BPR, Swing Factor otherwise |

**Why a one-team roster recovers the team's own belief, stated once so nobody re-derives it:** `momentsFor` undoes the even-split shrinkage by scaling the summed per-team variances by `roster.length² / contributing`. With `roster.length === 1` and one contributing belief that factor is exactly `1`, so the returned variance IS `varianceOf(belief)` and the returned mean IS `belief.mean`. A team with no belief for a variable yields mean `0` and variance `0` — the same honest cold start the alliance path already produces, and it is included in the field statistics as a zero rather than skipped, because a cold team really is part of the field.

**The all-or-nothing roster rule, reproduced exactly.** `buildFieldContributions` returns `null` if **any** roster team is missing from `consistencyByTeam()` or from `teamTotals`. This is `makeRankingPointFiller`'s existing rule and the comment above it explains the measured reason (2026-09-09, `2026isde4`): deciding per match priced the first synthetic match and then failed on a later one, which the builder correctly treats as corruption, taking the whole publish down. Decide once for the whole event; a `null` is the ordinary "we have not seen enough of this roster to price it" answer and the sidecar is skipped silently.

### Step 2 — the field statistics, computed PER EVENT

Over the `n` contributions (RESEARCH.md Open Question 2 — per event, never season-wide):

- `meanOfVariableMeans[v]` = arithmetic mean over the roster of `variableMeans[v]`
- `meanOfVariableVariances[v]` = arithmetic mean over the roster of `variableVariances[v]`
- `varianceOfVariableMeans[v]` = **population** variance over the roster of `variableMeans[v]` (divide by `n`, not `n-1` — this is the whole field, not a sample from a larger one; state that in the doc comment)
- `meanOfScoreMeans`, `varianceOfScoreMeans` = the same two quantities over `scoreMean`
- `meanOfBandVariances` = arithmetic mean over the roster of `bandVariance`

`varianceOfVariableMeans` and `varianceOfScoreMeans` are **the partner-quality spread**. They are what D-16 means by "capturing both match-to-match noise and partner-quality spread", and they are the term that a single-composition probe would miss — omitting them produces bands that are too narrow, which is the failure mode this construction exists to avoid.

### Step 3 — the field-averaged alliance moments

For team `t`, per variable `v`:

| Quantity | Team `t`'s alliance | The opposing alliance |
|---|---|---|
| `meanVector[v]` | `variableMeans[v] + (A-1) * meanOfVariableMeans[v]` | `A * meanOfVariableMeans[v]` |
| `varianceBlock[v][v]` | `A * (variableVariances[v] + (A-1) * meanOfVariableVariances[v]) + (A-1) * varianceOfVariableMeans[v]` | `A * (A * meanOfVariableVariances[v]) + A * varianceOfVariableMeans[v]` |
| `scoreMean` | `scoreMean + (A-1) * meanOfScoreMeans` | `A * meanOfScoreMeans` |
| `scoreVariance` | `bandVariance + (A-1) * meanOfBandVariances + (A-1) * varianceOfScoreMeans` | `A * meanOfBandVariances + A * varianceOfScoreMeans` |
| off-diagonal `varianceBlock[v][w]` | `0` | `0` |
| `scoreCrossCovariance[v]` | `0` | `0` |

Three things in that table are decisions rather than algebra and must be written into the module's doc comment so a later reader finds them instead of re-deriving them:

1. **The leading `A *` on every variance row is not a fudge factor.** It is `momentsFor`'s own even-split undo: a full `A`-team alliance's variance is `A` times the sum of its members' per-team variances (`varianceSum * A² / A`). The field-averaged form substitutes the field mean for the `A-1` unknown partners inside that same expression rather than around it.
2. **The composition-spread terms (`(A-1) * varianceOfVariableMeans`, `A * varianceOfVariableMeans`, and the score counterparts) treat the partner draw as Gaussian**, which D-16 names as the approximation class and explicitly accepts: "The composition-induced spread would be treated as Gaussian, the same approximation class used elsewhere." The exact mixture over all `C(n-1, 2)` partner pairs crossed with all opposing triples is computable and is not computed — it is roughly `5.7 million` `analyticRpPmf` calls per team on a 40-team roster.
3. **The score half rests on an additive-contribution assumption, and the constant cancels.** Under `allianceScore = sum of member totals + C`, the mean score difference for team `t`'s field-averaged match is `(scoreMean_t + (A-1)*meanOfScoreMeans + C) - (A*meanOfScoreMeans + C) = scoreMean_t - meanOfScoreMeans`: a team wins more than half its matches exactly when it is above field average, and any per-season additive constant drops out. This is **PLANNER ASSUMPTION A-FA1**, recorded in the Assumptions section below and **measured in Task 1** rather than asserted.

### Step 4 — the per-match pmf, the season total, and the rank inputs

`fieldAveragedMatchPmf` calls `analyticRpPmf({ red: own, blue: opponent, ruleModule, eventType, compLevel: "qm", config })` and returns `redPmf` — team `t`'s field-averaged distribution over its **own alliance's total RP in one qualification match**, length `ruleModule.maxRp + 1`.

`seasonTotalPmf(perMatchPmf, matchesPerTeam)` folds `convolvePmf` starting from `[1]`, `matchesPerTeam` times. This is D-16's "exact convolution of N copies of its field-averaged per-match pmf", and it is exact **given** the near-independence assumption the caveats name. Length is `matchesPerTeam * ruleModule.maxRp + 1`.

`fieldAveragedRankInputs(roster, perTeamPmf, matchesPerTeam)` builds the ranking arm's inputs — **one solo row per team**:

- `baselines`: `{ teamKey, earnedRpSum: 0, matchesPlayed: 0 }` for every roster team, exactly as the baked path already does ("nobody has played, which is exactly what 'before schedule release' means").
- `matches`: for each team, `{ redTeamKeys: [teamKey], blueTeamKeys: [], redRpPmf: seasonTotalPmf(...), blueRpPmf: [1] }`.

**Why a solo row is the right shape and not a hack, stated at the construction site.** `simulateRanks` ranks by average RP per match played. Giving each team one row carrying its whole-season total makes every team's `matchesPlayed` exactly `1`, so the average IS the season total and the ordering is identical to ranking by total — which is also what the baked arm produces, since every team there ends with the same `matchesPerTeam` credited matches (surrogates play an extra match and are excluded from the team-key lists, so they earn no extra credit). The lexicographic `teamKey` tie-break is unchanged, so seeded reproducibility is unchanged. The empty `blueTeamKeys` accumulates nothing and the `[1]` pmf is a valid single-outcome distribution that consumes one rng value — the cost of keeping `simulateRanks` untouched, and cheaper than the alternative of drawing `matchesPerTeam` rows per team, which is distributionally identical and `matchesPerTeam` times more draws.

**Determinism.** The artifact publishes its own `seed` (an FNV-1a hash of `eventKey|algorithmVersion|fieldAveraged`, the existing hashing convention in `preSchedule.ts`) and its own `draws`, so the offline arm, the measurement script and the browser all run `simulateRanks(matches, baselines, draws, mulberry32(seed))` on identical inputs and get identical histograms. That identity is a test, not a hope.

## The rung-1 acceptance criterion

**Quoted verbatim from `09-PLAN-OUTLINE.md` § "Answer to the open question — rung 1's pass/fail bar". Do not paraphrase it, do not re-derive it, and do not adjust a number in it after seeing a result.**

> **PLANNER ASSUMPTION (not from CONTEXT.md — recorded because D-17 rung 1 states its bar in prose
> and RESEARCH.md Open Question 1 / assumption A4 leave the statistic open).** Rung 1 passes when,
> measured with the **same `continuousQuantile` p10 / median / p90 rank statistics the live rank-band
> display already uses**, across a sample of **at least six real finished events spanning the 6–100
> team template coverage range, with at least one event per season 2022–2026 where one exists**:
>
> 1. **Median rank:** `|median_rung1 − median_baked| ≤ 0.5` ranks for **≥ 95%** of teams, and
>    `≤ 1.0` ranks for **every** team (no single-team outlier worse than one rank).
> 2. **Band edges:** `|p10_rung1 − p10_baked| ≤ 1.0` **and** `|p90_rung1 − p90_baked| ≤ 1.0` ranks
>    for **≥ 90%** of teams.
> 3. **No systematic shift:** the **mean signed** median-rank difference across all sampled teams is
>    within `±0.25` ranks.
>
> Both arms must be produced by the **same imported `simulateRanks`** and the same quantile helper —
> the arms differ only in the pmf inputs, never in the scorer (the `measureRewindGap.ts` convention,
> and the same-scorer discipline D-11 imposes on the RP side).

**Why these numbers, reproduced so nobody re-litigates them (outline, same section):** the settled display convention is a 10th-90th band printed to one decimal place, so `0.5` ranks is the smallest median difference the rendered page can show as distinct; and the sketch measurements found the middle 80% of a real rank distribution spans only **1-5 ranks of 17**, so a 1-rank band-edge error is already a material fraction of a typical band rather than a rounding artefact. The tolerance is deliberately in **rank units, not probability units**, because that is the unit a human reads off the page — an abstract distributional distance (Wasserstein/EMD) nobody will eyeball was considered and rejected.

**One recorded reading of clause (1), fixed before the run so it cannot be chosen afterwards.** "`≤ 0.5` for ≥ 95% of teams **and** `≤ 1.0` for every team" is evaluated **pooled across the whole sample**, not per event: a 6-event sample of 30-70 teams each gives roughly 250 teams, and a per-event 95% on a 22-team roster would round to "at most one team may miss", which is a materially stricter bar than the sentence says. The per-event breakdown is still printed, so a single bad event cannot hide inside the pool.

**Two things the criterion deliberately does not measure, named so their absence is a decision:** it says nothing about the two arms agreeing on *which* team holds a given rank (a permutation-level claim the bands themselves do not make), and it says nothing about either arm being *right* about the real event (that is the rewind-honesty question 08-08 already measured, and neither arm is validated against realised rankings here).
