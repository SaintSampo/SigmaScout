<objective>
Build the three deterministic district point formula modules and the one additive hook the
browser-side joint simulation needs, in `packages/core/districts` and
`packages/core/algorithms/simulation`. Nothing here renders, fetches, publishes or installs.

Purpose: every blue cell on the Road to District Champs tab is ultimately one of these four
numbers — qualification points from rank, alliance points from pick slot, playoff points from
bracket placement, award points (10-02's table) — and the per-draw ranking that correlates the
first three within a single simulated event. Plans 10-04, 10-06 and 10-07 all sit on top of this
file set. Getting a formula wrong here silently poisons every downstream number, which is exactly
why the corpus reconciliation test is written FIRST and why the point values are reconciled
against real TBA-reported rows rather than hand-typed fixtures.

Output: `qualPoints.ts`, `selectionPoints.ts`, `bracket.ts` (routing, placement points, and the
non-eight-alliance empirical fallback), each with pure unit tests; one corpus-backed
`pointFormulas.reconciliation.test.ts` proving all three against real data; and an optional
`onDraw` parameter on `simulateRanks` that leaves its existing callers and their measured
rewind-overconfidence oracle byte-identical.

**Run this plan in the main checkout, not a git worktree.** `data/corpus.sqlite` is gitignored and
does not travel into a worktree (project memory `project_worktree_gitignored_state`), and
`better-sqlite3`'s node-gyp build fails in a fresh worktree on this machine
(`project_pnpm_install_exit1_worktrees`). Without the corpus the reconciliation test skips, and a
green skip is not proof of anything — it is the failure mode this plan exists to prevent.
</objective>

**Fact 1 — playoff points are a placement table, not a per-round exit table.**
`10-RESEARCH.md` Code Example 5's routing topology is correct, but its round-to-points annotation
("M9/M10 loser OUT, 7 pts", "M12 loser OUT, 13 pts", "M13 loser OUT, 13 pts") is wrong: it
implies two alliances at 7 and two at 13. The corpus says otherwise. Across all 418 district-tier
eight-alliance events in 2023 through 2026, 408 show the exact placement multiset
`{30, 20, 13, 7, 0, 0, 0, 0}` — one alliance at each nonzero value, four at zero. Two sample
events checked directly (`2026wabon`, `2026casnv`) each show exactly three teams at each of 7, 13,
20 and 30 and the whole rest of the field at 0. The correct mapping is by final placement:
first 30, second 20, third 13, fourth 7, fifth through eighth 0. The value set 0/7/13/20/30 from
CONTEXT is right; the assignment is not.

The ten non-matching events are all explained and none contradicts the table: seven have one or
more alliances whose every pick is a non-district team absent from `district_rankings` (a short
list, right values), and three (`2026njtab`, `2026mawor`, `2026waahs`) carry a backup robot whose
proration puts a partial value in a pick slot.

**Fact 2 — in 2023-plus, the non-eight-alliance case is exclusively the divisioned district
championship parent event.** (Scope matters: Fact 5 below shows this does NOT hold before 2023.) Grouping `event_alliances` by event over every 2023-plus event with a non-null
`events.district_key` gives exactly three alliance counts: 8 (111, 115, 121, 144 events by
season), 4 (one event per season) and 2 (three events per season). Every single non-eight case is
a district championship parent (`event_type = 2`) that has divisions: `micmp` with its four
division winners, and `necmp` / `oncmp` / `txcmp` with their two each. Every division itself
(`event_type = 5`, e.g. `2023micmp1` through `2023micmp4`) carries eight alliances and the
standard bracket. So **every regular district event in 2023-plus uses the eight-alliance bracket,
without exception**, and the fallback table's real population is 16 parent events across four
seasons. `10-RESEARCH.md`'s hypothesis of "4- or 6-alliance district events with a shorter
bracket" was wrong about the cause.

A divisioned parent's own playoff points come from a different table than the eight-alliance one:
at `2023micmp` the raw `elim_points` observed were 0, 30 and 60, which at the 3x DCMP weight is
base 0, 10 and 20 — values absent from the eight-alliance set. That is precisely why a fabricated
bracket for these events would be wrong, and why CONTEXT calls for a measured table.

**Fact 3 — team-level proration exists and must be excluded explicitly, not silently.** An
alliance that used a backup robot splits one slot's points across two teams
(`2023vabla` alliance 1 reads 30, 30, 5, 25 for a placement of 30; `2023midet` alliance 3 reads
13, 13, 9, 5 for a placement of 13, which does not even sum to 3x13). Reconcile only alliances
whose `picks` array has exactly three entries, count the excluded four-robot alliances, and
assert that exclusion count so it cannot quietly grow.

**Fact 5 — the selection formula holds on all ten seasons too, but ONLY on eight-alliance events,

**Fact 6 — the corpus columns and shapes the test needs.**
`event_rankings(event_key, team_key, rank, total_teams)` is the rank-and-field-size source.
