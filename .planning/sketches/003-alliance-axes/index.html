Real 2025new predictions vs real results
The band widths on this page are wrong, and knowingly so. They are sqrt(sum of published component variances), which is not D-10's quantity. D-10 defines a match prediction's ± as the full predictive variance — estimate uncertainty plus performance spread (P + Q + R). The harness computes exactly that in linkFunctions.ts to produce pRedWin, but EventMatchSchema does not publish it — the artifact carries scores, pRedWin, and per-component mean/variance, and nothing else. So the widest honest band available to a client today is the wrong one.

It shows: in all three real matches below the actual result lands far outside the drawn band (SF10m1 red is ~7σ out, QM13 blue ~10σ). That is an artifact of the missing term, not evidence the model is broken — the win probabilities themselves are computed from the correct variance internally. The fix is a pipeline change, not a design change: publish match-level predictive variance on the event artifact. Judge the layout here, not the widths.

Both alliances on one shared axis. The overlap between the two bands is the win probability, drawn rather than asserted — heavy overlap means a coin flip, separation means a lock. Sharing one scale is what makes that readable, and it is why this is one axis rather than two: two independent scales would let equal-looking bands mean different things.

Costs vertical room and makes the overlap — the thing that explains the win probability — something you infer rather than see. Same data, different emphasis.
